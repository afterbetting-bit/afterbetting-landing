Chasing losses in gambling: why it never works

I once won €68,000. Then I spent years chasing it. What I learned.

By the founder of Afterbetting · 17 October 2026 · 8 min read

I once won €68,000.

That sounds like the end of a problem. It was the start of a bigger one.

In the period after, I gambled all of it away. Every euro. And for years after that I was chasing two things: that feeling, and the money I had lost.

Neither came back.

This is about chasing losses. Why you keep trying, why it does not work, and what you can do instead. Not a lecture. Just what I went through.

What chasing losses actually looks like

You lose. You want to undo it. So you play again.

Often with a bigger stake, so you get back quicker. Often faster, because it has to happen now. And often with money that was meant for something else.

The sentence in your head: "If I win this back, I will stop."

I thought that sentence hundreds of times. I never stopped when I got it back.

Why it does not work

The maths is against you

Gambling is built so the operator wins over time. If it were not, casinos and betting sites would not exist. You can win a night. You can win a month. But the longer you play, the more the result drifts towards the house.

Chasing means playing longer. Exactly the opposite of what you need.

Even if you win it back, you do not stop

This is the part I refused to see for the longest time.

Say it works. You win it all back. Then what?

I know what I did. I kept going. If it worked today, it could work tomorrow. And not just to get back to zero, but to get ahead.

The thought that changed everything for me: if I won it back, I would just gamble it again. Winning was never the way out. It was the fuel.

That €68,000 is my proof

If winning it back could ever have worked, it was then. I had more than enough. I could have walked away.

I did not. I just had a new goal: that feeling, one more time. And when the money was gone, a second goal arrived: winning it back.

A big win does not fix anything. It makes it worse. It gives your brain proof that it is possible, and that proof sticks around for years. I wrote more about that in how to stop gambling when you are winning.

Why your head says it can work

After a loss it often feels like you are "due". Like there is a balance that has to even out.

That balance does not exist. A card, a slot or a match has no idea what you lost before. Every round starts from scratch.

Then there is shame. A loss is not just money. It is also: how do I explain this? To my partner, to myself. Winning it back feels like a way to make it never have happened, so nobody has to know.

And there is the feeling itself. The moment you win. Your brain remembers that far better than all the nights you lost.

Signs you are chasing losses

  • You keep playing after a loss to "get back to even".
  • You raise your stakes to win it back faster.
  • You use bill money, or borrow, to keep playing.
  • You keep a running total in your head of what you still need to win back.
  • You think: "One more, then I stop."
  • After a win you do not stop. You keep going.

Recognise a few of these? You are not the only one. But it is a sign that gambling is in charge, not you.

Why a limit does not help when you are chasing

A lot of people try a limit first. A deposit limit, a maximum per night, a fixed amount per week.

For someone who chases losses, that rarely works. A limit becomes a target. You play until you hit it. And after a loss, the limit feels like the only thing standing between you and winning it back. So you raise it. Or open a second account. Or play somewhere else.

I am not saying limits are bad. For people who play now and then, they can help. But if you notice you are chasing, a limit is not the answer. The question is no longer how much you play, but whether you play.

That is why self-exclusion and blocking work better than limits. They take the choice away at the moment you are least able to make it. How to set that up is in how to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments.

Stop keeping your losses secret

Chasing is often about secrecy. You want to win it back before anyone notices. As long as nobody knows, you can still "fix" it.

I know how that feels. I also know the weight of that secret gets heavier every time you try to play it away.

Telling someone what is going on takes a lot of that pressure off. Not because the money comes back. Because you no longer have to gamble to hide something. And because someone can keep an eye on things with you when it gets hard.

It is one of the scariest conversations you can have. How to approach it is in how to tell someone you have a gambling problem.

What I did instead

Letting the money go

This was the hardest part. Accepting it was gone. Not gone for now. Gone.

As long as I was still going to "get it back" in my head, it was not really gone. And as long as it was not really gone, I kept playing.

Accepting the loss feels like losing. But it is the moment you stop losing.

Writing down the real number

I wrote down everything I owed. It was more than I thought. But from then on I had a number I could do something with. Not win back. Pay off.

Paying off is slower. Much slower. But it works. In how I paid off my gambling debt I explain how I did it in almost two years.

Stopping before you are back to even

Do not wait until you are "even" to stop. That moment does not come. Or if it does, you will not stop anyway.

Stop now. With the loss. That is the only place stopping actually happens.

If you have just lost a lot

Maybe you are reading this right after a bad night. Here is what I would tell you.

  • Do not play again today. Not even "one more". Especially not one more.
  • Put the money out of reach. Move what is left to an account you cannot easily get to, or let someone you trust hold the keys for a while.
  • Switch on a block. Self-exclude through GamStop in the UK or Cruks in the Netherlands, and block gambling on your phone and bank card. Make tomorrow harder for yourself.
  • Call or text someone. You do not have to tell them everything. Just do not sit alone with this.
  • Wait before deciding anything. The urge is big right now. In an hour it will be smaller. Read do gambling urges go away for what to do in the meantime.

Grieving the money

Nobody tells you this. You are allowed to be gutted about the money. Properly gutted.

For a long time I acted like it was "just money". Or like I was going to get it back. Both were ways of not feeling the loss.

It is gone. That hurts. You could have done things with it. You are allowed to feel that.

And after that, you are allowed to move on. Not by winning back the past, but by building something new. That starts with one evening of not playing. And then another.

Finally

I won €68,000 and chased the feeling for years. It only cost me more.

What got me out was not a big win. It was giving up on winning it back.

The money will not come back through a slot machine. But your life can come back. Differently, slower, and for real.

Frequently asked questions

Can you win back gambling losses?

Sometimes in the short term, but chasing means playing longer, and over time the operator wins. People who do win it back usually keep playing. The only place you can really stop is with the loss.

Why do I keep chasing my losses?

Because it feels like you are due a win, because you want to undo the damage before anyone notices, and because your brain remembers a past win far better than all the times you lost.

What should I do after losing a lot of money gambling?

Do not play again today, put the money out of reach, switch on a block and talk to someone. In the UK the National Gambling Helpline is free and open 24/7 on 0808 8020 133.
