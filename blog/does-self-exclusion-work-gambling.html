Does self-exclusion actually work for gambling addiction?

Self-exclusion is the first thing most people are told to do. It helps. But it is not enough on its own, and here is why.

By Afterbetting · 7 min read

If you have sought help for gambling addiction, you have almost certainly been pointed towards self-exclusion. In the UK it is GamStop. In the Netherlands it is CRUKS. Most countries have a national register or casino-level exclusion programme. The question is: does it actually work?

The honest answer is: it helps, and it is worth doing, but it is not the solution. It is one tool in a set of tools that need to work together.

What self-exclusion does well

Self-exclusion removes access from licensed, regulated gambling operators. For people whose primary gambling was with major bookmakers or casinos, this creates a genuine barrier. When you are excluded and try to log in, you cannot. That moment of friction, that pause between impulse and action, can be enough to stop a bet that would otherwise have happened.

It is not theatre. When you are excluded and try to log in, you cannot. The barrier is real, even if it is not complete.

Self-exclusion also serves a psychological function. It is a public commitment. Signing up requires acknowledging the problem to someone, even if that someone is a website. That act of acknowledgment matters. If you have just had a heavy loss and need to act tonight, start here: what to do after you have gambled away your paycheck.

Where self-exclusion falls short

Self-exclusion only covers the operators you exclude from. It does not cover unregulated offshore sites, crypto casinos, friends who take bets, or in-person gambling. For people with severe addiction, finding a way around exclusion is not difficult, it is almost automatic.

More fundamentally, self-exclusion addresses access but not the underlying drive. The craving that made you want to bet is still present after you are excluded. Without replacing the behaviour and building new patterns, the excluded gambler often finds new routes.

What works better alongside self-exclusion

Complete device-level blocking

Tools like Gamban block gambling sites across all devices and browsers, including apps. This is a more complete barrier than operator-level exclusion alone and is harder to circumvent in a moment of weakness.

Daily structure that fills the gambling time

The hours you used to gamble need to be filled with something intentional. Building habits that replace gambling is the most durable protection against relapse because it addresses the emotional need that gambling was meeting.

Financial accountability

Giving a trusted person oversight of your finances, or using financial tracking tools that make your spending visible, removes the secrecy that enables continued gambling. Should you self-exclude?

Yes. Do it today if you have not already. It is not a cure but it is a meaningful layer of protection. The question is not whether to self-exclude but what you build around it.

Recovery from gambling addiction is a structural challenge, not a willpower challenge. The more barriers between you and a bet, and the more your time is filled with alternatives, the less your willpower has to carry alone.

How long self-exclusion lasts, and what happens when it ends

In the UK, GamStop lets you choose six months, one year or five years. In the Netherlands, Cruks has a minimum of six months. The rules on ending it early are strict on purpose, because the moment you want out is usually the moment you most need it.

If you are thinking about cancelling, read can you cancel GamStop early first. It explains the rules honestly, and what to do if the urge is strong right now.

What to do on the day you self-exclude

Self-exclusion works best when you do it as part of a short list on the same day, not as a single action.

  • Sign up for GamStop or Cruks. It takes a few minutes.
  • Install blocking software on every device. Phone, laptop, tablet. Exclusion covers licensed sites. Blocking covers much more.
  • Switch on your bank's gambling block. Many UK banks have one. How it works per bank is in how to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments.
  • Unsubscribe from every gambling email and delete the apps. Every notification is an invitation.
  • Tell one person. Not to be watched, but so you are not the only one who knows.
  • Plan your first evening. The first evening after excluding is often harder than you expect. Decide now what you will do.

If you have already found a way around it

Many people find a site that is not covered. If that is you, you are not the only one, and it does not mean self-exclusion was pointless. It means you need more layers, not fewer. Read found a casino not on GamStop? for what to do tonight and tomorrow.

What self-exclusion cannot do for you

It cannot fill the hours you used to gamble. It cannot answer the feeling that comes up on a Friday night. It cannot pay off a debt or rebuild trust.

For me, the routine was what carried the weight. Fixed things at fixed times, especially in the evenings and at weekends. The blocks bought me time. The routine is what I did with that time. More on that in how to stop gambling urges at night and on weekends.
