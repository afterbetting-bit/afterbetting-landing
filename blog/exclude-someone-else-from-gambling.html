Can you exclude someone else from gambling?

What you can do, what you cannot, and what to try first.

By Afterbetting · 20 October 2026 · 7 min read

Someone you love gambles. You have talked, pleaded, maybe threatened. Nothing changes. And now you are searching for a way to block them yourself.

That is not betrayal. It is what people do when they watch someone go under.

I gambled for 8 years. I know how good you get, as a gambler, at explaining why it is not that bad. And I know from my mum what it is like on the other side. She looked for help for herself and found almost nothing. Everything was about the gambler, not the people around them.

This article is for you, the person watching. What you can do, what you cannot, and what to try first.

The short answer

In the UK, no. You cannot sign someone else up to GamStop. GamStop says it plainly: you must not try to register someone else. The only exception is when you are legally authorised to act for them, for example with a valid Power of Attorney.

The same goes for self-exclusion in betting shops, arcades and casinos. It is called self-exclusion for a reason. The person who gambles has to do it.

Some countries do allow it. In the Netherlands, a family member can ask the regulator to exclude someone for six months. But in Great Britain, the decision sits with them.

That can feel like a dead end. It is not. There is a lot you can do.

What you can do today

Register yourself on GamStop. It sounds odd, but GamStop suggests it. It stops your own name and details from being used to open gambling accounts. If money or cards have gone missing, this closes one door.

Protect your money. You do not have to wait for them to be ready.

  • Keep your income in an account in your own name.
  • Use one joint account only for fixed bills.
  • Do not lend money or co-sign anything while they are still gambling.
  • If you have joint accounts or joint debts, get free advice from StepChange or National Debtline about where you stand.

Turn on the gambling block on your own cards. Most big UK banks have one in their app. If your partner uses your card, or a joint card, that matters.

Then: ask them to do it themselves

Self-exclusion they choose is stronger anyway. They pick the length, at least six months. And it is their decision, which makes for a very different conversation than one that is forced on them.

How to ask:

  • Pick a calm moment. Not in the middle of a row, not right after money has gone.
  • Say what you see, not what they are. "I see money going every month" lands better than "you are an addict".
  • Ask for one concrete thing. "Will you sign up to GamStop tonight? I will sit with you."
  • Accept that the answer might be no. The first conversation is rarely the last.

If they say yes, help them add more locks at the same time: a blocker on their phone and the gambling block in their banking app. How to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments has the steps.

More on that conversation in My partner has a gambling problem.

Expect the lies, and do not take them as the whole truth

They may say it is under control. They may say it was a one-off. Lying is part of how a gambling problem keeps going. It is not proof that they do not care about you. Why gambling addicts lie explains how small lies grow.

That does not mean you have to believe them. Go by what you see: money, times, moods. Not by what you are told.

What exclusion does not do

Even if they sign up, GamStop is one lock. It covers sites licensed in Great Britain. Sites without that licence do not check it. Found a casino not on GamStop? explains what that means.

So whatever they decide, your own money and your own support stay just as important.

And you?

My mum struggled with it most. She found little help for herself. I would like to spare you that.

Call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133 and say you are calling as a family member. It is free and open 24/7. GamFam and Gam-Anon run groups for families too. More options in Where to get help for gambling addiction.

You cannot make someone stop. You can make sure you do not go under with them. That is not giving up. That is love too.

Frequently asked questions

Can I self-exclude someone else from gambling?

Not in the UK. GamStop and other self-exclusion schemes have to be set up by the person who gambles. The only exception is when you are legally authorised to act for them, for example with a valid Power of Attorney.

Can I put my husband on GamStop?

No. He has to register himself. What you can do is register yourself, so your details cannot be used for gambling accounts, protect your own money and ask him to sign up, ideally with you sitting next to him.

Can I stop my partner using our joint account for gambling?

You cannot block their gambling for them, but you can move your income to an account in your own name and keep the joint account for fixed bills only. Ask your bank about the options for joint accounts.

What if they refuse to self-exclude?

Keep protecting yourself and your money, and get support for yourself. Call the National Gambling Helpline as a family member. Many people need more than one conversation before they are ready.
