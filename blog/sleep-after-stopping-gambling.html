Can't sleep after quitting gambling? What helps

Why the nights are hard after stopping, and what you can change from tonight.

By Afterbetting · updated 11 October 2026 · 7 min read

You stopped gambling. You expected to feel better. Instead you are lying awake at 2am, wide awake, with your thoughts going round and round.

You are not imagining it, and you are not alone. Bad nights are one of the most common things people describe in the first weeks after stopping. The good news is that this is one of the problems you can do something about, tonight.

Why the nights are hard after stopping

There are a few reasons, and most of them have to do with how gambling used to fill your evenings.

Your nights were built around gambling. Late matches, in-play markets, casino sessions after everyone else had gone to bed. For many people the night was the main gambling window. Your body and your habits got used to being switched on at that time. Stopping does not reset that overnight.

The quiet gives your worries room. During the day there is work, people, noise. At night it is just you and your thoughts. Money, the conversations you still need to have, what you lost. It all gets louder in the dark.

Your phone is still in your hand. The device you used to gamble on is often the last thing you look at before sleep. Even without gambling apps, it keeps your head busy and keeps the old routine alive.

The urge often comes late. If you used to gamble at night, that is also when the urge is most likely to come back.

What it was like for me

From the founder: Sleep was one of the first things that improved when I stopped. Within two to three weeks, I was sleeping longer and more deeply than I had in years. The 3am checking of live scores, the lying awake thinking about odds, the financial anxiety at night. All of it eased faster than I expected.

That was my experience. Yours may be faster or slower. What helped was treating the evening as part of recovery, not as the time after it.

What helps, starting tonight

Get the phone out of the bedroom

This is the single change that helps most people. Charge your phone in another room. Buy a cheap alarm clock if you need one. You remove both the access and the habit of checking.

If that feels too big, start smaller: phone on the other side of the room, face down, an hour before you want to sleep.

Give the evening an ending

A fixed wind-down tells your body the day is over. It does not have to be special. The same few things in the same order:

  1. Phone away.
  2. Something calm: a shower, a book, music, a short walk.
  3. Three lines on paper: what went well today, what was hard, what you will do tomorrow.
  4. Lights out at roughly the same time.

The NHS advice for poor sleep says the same thing in its own words: wind down for at least an hour before bed, avoid screens right before bed, and keep to a regular schedule.

Get up at the same time every day

Even after a bad night. Even at the weekend. It feels wrong, but sleeping in after a bad night tends to make the next night worse. A fixed time to get up is often what pulls your sleep back into rhythm.

Write the worries down before bed

If money is what keeps you awake, give it a place during the day. A short list of what you owe and one next step does more for your nights than lying awake doing the sums in your head. Rebuilding your finances after gambling has a plan you can start with.

At night, if a worry comes, write it on a piece of paper next to your bed. It is still there in the morning. You do not have to hold it all night.

Be careful with what you use to switch off

Alcohol might make you sleepy, but it tends to make sleep worse later in the night. The NHS also advises avoiding caffeine, nicotine and alcohol in the hours before bed, and big meals late at night.

Plan for the urge at night

If the urge comes at 1am, have a plan that does not involve your phone. Get up, go to another room, drink a glass of water, put on a light, wait it out. Urges rise and then fall. A 15-minute timer helps. More on getting through those moments in How to stop gambling urges at the weekend.

Watch what helps you

Everyone is different. What helps one person sleep does nothing for someone else. So keep it simple and watch for a couple of weeks.

Each morning, note two things: how you slept, and what you did the evening before. Phone in the bedroom or not. Exercise or not. Late caffeine or not. After a couple of weeks, you will usually see a pattern. That pattern is your own sleep plan.

When to get help

See your GP if:

  • you have been sleeping badly for months,
  • changing your evening routine has not helped,
  • or the bad nights make it hard to cope during the day.

If the nights bring dark thoughts as well as worries, do not wait for the morning. In the UK you can call Samaritans on 116 123, free, day or night.

Tonight

You do not need to fix your sleep this week. You only need to change tonight a little.

Phone in another room. One calm thing before bed. Three lines on paper. Up at the same time tomorrow.

That is enough for tonight.

Frequently asked questions

Why can I not sleep after I stopped gambling?

Many people gambled in the evening or at night, so their routine and body are used to being switched on then. Without gambling, the quiet also gives worries about money and relationships more room. Having your phone in bed keeps the old routine going.

What helps with sleep after quitting gambling?

Charge your phone outside the bedroom, give the evening a fixed wind-down, get up at the same time every day and write worries down before bed. The NHS also advises avoiding caffeine, alcohol and screens right before bed.

When should I see a GP about sleep?

The NHS advises seeing a GP if you have slept badly for months, if changing your habits has not helped, or if poor sleep makes it hard to cope with daily life.
