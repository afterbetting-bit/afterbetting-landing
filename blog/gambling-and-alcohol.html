Gambling and alcohol: why drinking makes the urge worse

Why the two feed each other, and how to set your locks before you drink.

By Afterbetting · 20 October 2026 · 7 min read

For me, alcohol made the urge to gamble worse. Not a little. Clearly.

I am not the only one. Under a post I wrote online about my recovery, the most upvoted reply was about exactly this.

This article is about why the two go together so well, and what you can do if you recognise it in yourself.

Why drinking and gambling feed each other

You do not need to be a scientist to see this. Alcohol lowers your inhibitions. You think less about tomorrow. That is why people drink at a party.

But a gambling problem is all about inhibition. About that one moment between the thought "I will just put something on" and opening the app. Sober, you can sometimes still catch that moment. A few drinks in, it is often gone before you notice.

On top of that:

  • Your limit moves. The stake you would never place sober suddenly feels normal after a few pints.
  • Your promises to yourself blur. "Just tonight" is easier to say after a drink.
  • The buzz feels bigger. Drinking and gambling both give you a rush. Together it feels like a party, until the next morning.
  • The morning after is harder. A hangover plus a loss is a bad mix. That is exactly when you want to win it back.

Do you recognise the pattern?

Ask yourself these questions. Honestly, nobody is watching.

  • Do I gamble more often, or more, when I have been drinking?
  • Were my biggest losses on nights I was drinking?
  • Do I drink to make gambling more exciting, or to forget a loss?
  • Have I been drinking more since I stopped gambling?

That last one matters. When you stop gambling, something falls away. The buzz, the distraction, a way to get through the evening. It is understandable to want to fill that gap. Watch that it does not quietly become drink.

What you can do

Do not drink in your risk hours. For me those were evenings and weekends. That is when there is time and nobody is watching. Drinking at those times is fuel on the fire. Not sure what your risk hours are? What to do instead of gambling helps you plan them.

Set your locks before you drink. That is the whole point. You make the decision sober, so you cannot undo it after a few drinks.

  • A gambling blocker on your phone and laptop. Choose one you cannot switch off in a few taps.
  • The gambling block in your banking app. Most UK banks build in a delay before you can turn it off.
  • GamStop, if you have not signed up yet.

How to set them up: How to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments.

Leave the cards at home. Going out? Take only what you need for the night.

Tell one person this is your weak spot. Someone who is there when you drink. "If I get my phone out for a bet after a few pints, say something." It feels awkward. It works.

Be honest about football and the pub. Watching the match with a pint puts two things together that make your urge bigger. Watch with people who know you have stopped. Or skip it for the first while.

If it goes wrong anyway

Drank and gambled? Then tomorrow you will probably wake up with shame, a hangover and a loss. That is a dangerous morning.

Do this:

  1. Make no decisions about money until you are sober and rested.
  2. Do not try to win it back. That is the thought that costs the most. More on that in Chasing losses.
  3. Tell someone. Saying out loud what happened takes some of the shame away.
  4. Look at what you can change for next time. Which lock was missing?

Read What to do right after a gambling relapse too. A relapse does not put you back at zero.

Getting help for both

Do you notice your drinking becoming a problem too? Or that you cannot see it separately from the gambling any more? Then it makes sense to tackle both, with someone who knows both.

  • Your GP can talk it through with you and refer you.
  • The NHS has gambling clinics and local alcohol services.
  • The National Gambling Helpline (0808 8020 133) is free and open 24/7.

More options in Where to get help for gambling addiction.

What I want you to take away

You do not have to give up drinking forever right now. That is your call, and something you can talk through with a professional. But know what drink does to your urge. And make your plans while you are sober.

For me it was clear: with drink in me, the urge got bigger. Knowing that is the first step. Acting on it is the second.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I gamble more when I drink?

Alcohol lowers your inhibitions. The moment where you would stop yourself sober is often gone after a few drinks. Your limits also shift: stakes you would never place sober start to feel normal.

Should I stop drinking if I stop gambling?

That is up to you. If drink makes your urge bigger, at least avoid drinking in your risk hours and set your blocks before you drink. If you wonder whether your drinking is a problem in itself, talk to your GP.

I drink more since I stopped gambling. Is that normal?

It is common for people to fill the gap gambling leaves with something else. It is good that you noticed. Talk to someone about it, for example your GP.

How do I stop gambling when drunk?

Do it before you drink. Turn on a blocker and your bank gambling block, leave your cards at home and tell someone who is with you that this is your weak spot.
