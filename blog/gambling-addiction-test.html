Gambling addiction test: am I addicted to gambling?

Nine questions about the last 12 months. Anonymous, two minutes.

By the founder of Afterbetting · 18 October 2026 · 2 min test, 7 min read

If you are reading this, you probably already have a feeling. Maybe you have had it for a while.

I know how that goes. I gambled for 8 years. I knew something was wrong long before I was willing to admit it. It did not happen all at once. It crept up on me. Every step felt small enough to ignore.

This test helps you look at it honestly. Nine questions about the last 12 months. It takes two minutes. Your answers are not stored anywhere, not by us and not by anyone else. Only you see your result.

Think about the last 12 months.

1/9Have you bet more than you could really afford to lose?
2/9Have you needed to gamble with larger amounts of money to get the same feeling of excitement?
3/9When you gambled, did you go back another day to try to win back the money you lost?
4/9Have you borrowed money or sold anything to get money to gamble?
5/9Have you felt that you might have a problem with gambling?
6/9Has gambling caused you any health problems, including stress or anxiety?
7/9Have people criticised your betting or told you that you had a gambling problem, whether or not you thought it was true?
8/9Has your gambling caused any financial problems for you or your household?
9/9Have you felt guilty about the way you gamble or what happens when you gamble?

Your answers stay on your device. We store nothing.

How this test works

The questions come from the PGSI, the Problem Gambling Severity Index. It was developed in Canada (Ferris and Wynne, 2001) and is used around the world, including by the Gambling Commission in Great Britain to measure gambling harm.

You answer each question with never, sometimes, most of the time or almost always. That gives 0, 1, 2 or 3 points. Your score is somewhere between 0 and 27.

A few things to know:

  • It is about the last 12 months. Not your whole life, and not just last week.
  • It is not a diagnosis. Only a doctor or a qualified professional can tell you whether you have a gambling disorder. The test shows how much risk you are carrying, and whether it makes sense to get help.
  • Honesty is the only thing that counts. Nobody is watching. There is no one here you need to convince.

What your score means

0: no problems with gambling. You do not gamble, or you gamble without it costing you anything in the last 12 months. Keep an eye on the signs anyway. Gambling problems almost always start small.

1 to 2: low risk. There are a few signs, but few consequences so far. This is the point where cutting back or stopping is easiest. Look at which question gave you points. That is your first warning.

3 to 7: moderate risk. Gambling is already affecting your money, your mood or the people around you. This is not something to wait out. Talking to someone really helps now, and you can do it anonymously.

8 or more: problem gambling. Your gambling is causing clear problems. That says nothing about who you are as a person. It does say it is time to get help. You do not have to do it alone, and you do not have to wait until it gets worse.

Why you often know the answer already

Question 5 says the most: have you felt that you might have a problem with gambling?

For me, the answer was yes for years. I knew. I just never said it out loud, not even to myself. There was always a reason to put it off. I would stop after a big win. After this month. Once I had won back what I lost.

If I had filled in this test honestly back then, I would have answered almost always on 7 of the 9 questions. That is a score of at least 21. I did not need a test to know. I needed something that made it impossible to brush off.

Maybe that is true for you too. Then this test is not a discovery. It is the answer written down. That can be confronting. It can also be a relief, because you no longer have to pretend.

The questions that say the most

Every question counts the same. Still, there are three I would look at twice.

Betting more than you can afford to lose (question 1). Gambling with money that was meant for rent, groceries or bills. I once gambled away my whole salary in one evening. In the moment it feels like borrowing from yourself. Looking back, it is where the hole starts. If that is where you are now, read gambled away your paycheck? What now.

Going back another day to win it back (question 3). This may be the clearest sign of all. You are no longer gambling for the thrill, but to put something right. I was stuck there for years. Why chasing losses never works, and what goes on in your head, is in chasing losses in gambling.

Borrowing or selling things to gamble (question 4). Once you are arranging money so you can gamble, it is no longer a night out. It is something you need. This is where debt grows fastest. I ended up more than €40,000 in debt. How I paid it back in almost two years is in how I paid off my gambling debt.

What to do now

If your score is low

Notice what changes. How often you gamble, how much you stake, why you start. If it creeps up, or you notice you gamble to push something away, take the test again in a few months.

If your score is moderate or high

Start small. You do not have to change your whole life today.

  • Talk to someone. In the UK, call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. In the US, call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738). Both are free, confidential and open 24/7. You do not need to be sure you are addicted to call.
  • Make gambling harder. Self-exclude, block gambling sites on every device and switch on your bank's gambling block. How that works is in how to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments.
  • Tell one person. Not so they can watch you. So you are not the only one who knows.
  • Look at your money. Write down honestly how much you have lost and what you owe. It is heavy, but it is where control starts.
  • Plan your hard hours. For me, that was evenings and weekends. Fixed things at fixed times helped me more than anything.

If you feel the urge while reading this

That happens more often than you would think. A test about gambling can bring the gambling up. Put your phone down for a moment, stand up, drink some water. An urge rises, peaks and fades. What to do in that moment is in do gambling urges go away?

If you are filling this in for someone else

Maybe you are worried about a partner, a child or a friend. You can use the questions to put your worries in order. You only get a real result if the person answers honestly themselves.

Do not use the test as evidence in a conversation. It will feel like an interrogation. Talk about what you see and what it does to you instead. More in my partner has a gambling problem.

Frequently asked questions

Is this gambling addiction test reliable?

The questions come from the PGSI, a questionnaire that has been used around the world for more than twenty years. It is a good first check. It is not a diagnosis. If you are unsure about your result, talk to a professional or your doctor.

Is my result stored?

No. The test runs only in your own browser. We do not see your answers and we store nothing.

I have a high score. Does that mean I am addicted?

The test says your gambling is causing clear problems. Only a qualified professional can tell you whether you have a gambling disorder. What matters most is not the word, but what you do next.

I only bet on sports. Does that count?

Yes. The questions cover every kind of gambling: sports betting, online casino, lotteries and scratch cards. I was a sports bettor for 8 years myself.

Where to get help for gambling addiction

In crisis right now? UK: National Gambling Helpline 0808 8020 133 (free, 24/7). US: 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), call or text, 24/7. Canada: your provincial gambling helpline. Thinking about suicide? UK: Samaritans 116 123. US and Canada: call or text 988. Netherlands: OpenOverGokken 0800-2400022. Elsewhere, or in danger: your local emergency number.
