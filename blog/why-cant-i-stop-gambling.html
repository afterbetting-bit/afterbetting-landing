Quitting

Why can't I stop gambling?

It isn't a flaw in your character. Eight reasons quitting is so hard, and what actually works.

By Afterbetting · 31 October 2026 · 7 min read

You've promised yourself. Maybe ten times. You deleted the app, you said "this is the last time", and you meant it. A few days later you were placing bets again.

Then comes the question that hurts more than any loss: why can't I do this?

I gambled for 8 years, mostly on sports. I started at 16. I asked myself that question hundreds of times. The answer isn't that you're weak. The answer is that you're fighting several things at once, and most people only bring one weapon: willpower.

Here are eight reasons quitting is so hard. Then what actually works.

1. Gambling gives you something nothing else does

Gambling isn't just putting money down. It's tension, hope and a few minutes of not thinking about anything else. For a moment life is simple: win or lose.

When you stop, that goes away. What's left is often emptiness. No buzz, no hope of that one big night, just an ordinary day. It doesn't feel like progress. It feels like losing something.

That's one reason people go back. Not because they want to lose, but because the emptiness feels worse than the gambling. More on what's going on in your head in what happens to your brain when you stop gambling.

2. You believe one good night will fix it

For me this was the strongest one. Every time I lost, the same thought came: one good night and this is fixed. The debt, the lies, the mess.

So losses never gave me a reason to stop. They gave me a reason to keep going. In my head I wasn't gambling anymore, I was "fixing it".

It took me a long time to see that it doesn't work like that. Even if I'd won it all back, I wouldn't have stopped. That money would have gone straight back in. More on this in chasing losses.

3. You've won before

I once won €68,000. At the time I thought it was the best day of my life. Looking back, it was the start of years of chasing that feeling.

I gambled the whole win away, and more after that. A big win proves to your brain that it's possible. After that, no loss can convince you it can't happen, because you've seen it with your own eyes.

Have you had a big win? It might be exactly what's keeping you hooked.

4. You're using willpower against empty hours

Most people try to quit by simply not doing it. Delete the apps, make promises, grit your teeth.

That never lasted for me. What I only noticed later: I almost never gambled during the day. Work gave the day a shape. It went wrong in the evenings and at weekends, the hours with nothing planned.

Willpower has to win those hours again and again. Nobody can keep that up. What did work for me was filling them: fixed things at fixed times, exercise on the nights I used to bet, reading. Boring, honestly. But it worked better than anything I'd tried before. How to do that is in daily habits that replace gambling.

5. It's in your pocket

You used to have to go to a casino or a betting shop. Now the casino is on your phone. Open day and night, nobody sees you walk in, a deposit in two taps.

That means every urge can turn into a bet straight away. There's no moment in between to think.

This isn't about character. It's about friction. Without friction, quitting is much harder than it needs to be. How to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments shows what you can set up on your phone, laptop and bank card. In the UK, GamStop excludes you from all licensed online operators.

6. You're carrying it alone

Gambling is easy to hide. No smell, no slurred speech. Many people keep it secret for years, even from their partner.

I told people everything was fine. That it was just a distraction. When I couldn't come along to something, I had a reason. The real reason was that there was no money.

As long as nobody knows, nobody can stop you, and there's nobody to turn to when the urge hits. Shame keeps gambling going. You don't have to tell everyone. One person you trust is enough. How to tell your family about your gambling problem walks you through that conversation.

7. The debt pulls you back

Debt isn't only a result of gambling. It becomes a reason to keep going. Every reminder letter, every bill you can't pay, brings the same thought: if I win now, it's gone.

In the end I owed more than €40,000. It only started shrinking when I stopped looking for the shortcut and paid a fixed amount every month. That took almost two years. If you have debt and want to stop, read how to pay off gambling debt and rebuild your finances.

8. Sometimes there's more going on

Sometimes gambling is a way of not feeling something else. Stress, low mood, loneliness, boredom. Alcohol often plays a part too: for me, drinking made the urge louder.

If something else is going on besides the gambling, stopping the gambling alone may not be enough. Then it makes sense to get help with it. I had a few months of professional help myself. It was one of the things that made the difference. See where to get help for gambling addiction.

What causes gambling addiction?

There isn't one cause. For most people several things come together:

  • Starting young. I started at 16. What starts early gets woven into how you deal with tension and boredom.
  • Easy access. A phone, a bank card and an app. There's almost no barrier left.
  • An early big win. It sets an expectation that never comes true again.
  • Emptiness or stress. Gambling fills something, or numbs something.
  • Secrecy. The longer it stays hidden, the bigger it gets.

You don't need to know the exact cause to stop. But it helps to see it isn't a flaw in your character. It's a pattern, and patterns can be broken.

What actually works

If willpower alone isn't enough, what is? This is what made the difference for me and for many others.

  1. Add friction. Block gambling sites and apps, switch on the gambling block at your bank, sign up to GamStop. Every barrier buys you a few minutes to think.
  2. Fill your risky hours. Know when you gamble? Plan something there. Exercise, call someone, a fixed walk. It doesn't have to be fun. It has to be there.
  3. Tell one person. Someone who knows makes hiding harder and the urge lighter.
  4. Look at the real number. Write down what you owe and what you've lost. A number can be dealt with. A fog can't.
  5. Let go of the shortcut. There's no bet that fixes it. Not even that one.
  6. Get help if you can't do it alone. That isn't failure. In the UK, call the National Gambling Helpline free on 0808 8020 133. In the US, call 1-800-MY-RESET.

If you've relapsed

I didn't stop in one go. After one of my first attempts I relapsed. That happens to a lot of people, and it doesn't mean it will never work.

A relapse doesn't wipe everything out. The days you didn't gamble were real. What you learned about your triggers stays. What matters most is what you do next. Read what to do after a gambling relapse for the first hours and days.

I've now been gambling-free for more than two years. Not because I suddenly got stronger, but because I stopped just fighting and started shaping my days differently.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I stop gambling even though I really want to?

Because wanting isn't the same as being able to. Gambling gives you something you miss when you stop, it's always within reach, and the thought that one win will fix everything keeps coming back. Friction, filled hours and support from another person help more than willpower alone.

Is it normal not to manage it the first time?

Yes. Many people need several attempts. I relapsed after one of my early attempts too. A relapse doesn't mean you can't do it.

What causes gambling addiction?

There isn't one cause. Starting young, easy access, a big win, stress or emptiness, and secrecy often come together.

Can I stop gambling on my own, without help?

Some people can, especially with barriers in place and a steady routine. If it hasn't worked after a few attempts, or there's more going on like low mood or debt, get help. A first call to a gambling helpline is free and confidential.

Help me stop gambling. Where do I start today?

With one barrier: block the app or site you use most, or sign up to GamStop. Then tell one person what's going on. More in [how to stop gambling](/blog/how-to-stop-gambling).
