Gambling addiction support groups, forums and meetings

Gamblers Anonymous, GamCare forums and chatrooms: what exists, what it costs and what to watch for.

By Afterbetting · 31 October 2026 · 5 min read

When you gamble, you often feel like the only one. Your friends don't get it, your family can't know, and a counsellor feels like too big a step. So you search for "gambling addiction forum" or "support groups near me". You want to read that someone else is going through the same thing.

That's a good instinct. Talking to people who've been there takes away part of the shame. You don't have to explain anything. They already understand.

This article sets out what's available in the UK and online, what it costs, and what to watch for.

Gamblers Anonymous (GA)

Gamblers Anonymous is the best-known support group for gambling. It started in Los Angeles in 1957 and works with twelve steps, like Alcoholics Anonymous.

In the UK:

  • Meetings: GA says it has meetings every day of the week across England, Wales and Ulster. You don't need an appointment, you can just turn up. There's a "find a meeting" tool on gamblersanonymous.org.uk.
  • Types of meetings: newcomers, mixed (family and friends welcome), women's, LGBTQ+, step meetings and open meetings.
  • Phone: 0330 094 0322.
  • Sponsors: new members often get a sponsor, someone who has been free from gambling for longer and helps them through the first year.
  • For family: Gam-Anon is the sister group for partners, family and friends (gamanon.org.uk).

In the US and many other countries GA meetings run too. Search for Gamblers Anonymous with your city or state.

The twelve-step approach suits some people very well and others less so. Research suggests people often do better when they combine meetings with other help, such as therapy.

GamCare: forum, chatrooms and groups

GamCare runs the National Gambling Helpline (0808 8020 133, free, 24/7). It also offers support you can use without picking up the phone:

  • An online forum: moderated, for peer support, where you write under a username instead of your real name.
  • Daily group chatrooms: online, with a moderator. There are also themed sessions with experts.
  • Monthly chatrooms for family and friends, and for women.
  • Recovery courses in a group: six to eight weeks, face to face or online.
  • Live chat and WhatsApp through the helpline.

All of it is free. For many people the forum or a chatroom is the first time they say it out loud, even if it's typed.

Online forums: what to watch for

Besides GamCare, there are other forums, subreddits and groups about gambling. Some are excellent, some aren't. A few things to watch for.

What a forum does well: - You read that others went through the same thing. That takes away shame. - You can write anonymously, at any time of day or night. - You read what worked for others.

What to watch for: - Triggers. Some posts go into detail about bets, wins and sites. That can stir the urge. If you notice it, stop reading. - Tips to get around blocks. In some places people share casinos that aren't on GamStop. That doesn't help you. If you notice yourself searching that way, read found a casino not on GamStop? Read this first. - Advice that sounds certain. A forum is mostly other people's experience. What worked for one person may not work for you. For medical or money questions, go to a professional. - Moderation. A moderated forum is safer than an open group in a messaging app.

What do you say the first time?

The first step is often the hardest. You don't need to prepare a story. A few sentences are enough.

When you go to a meeting or call a group: "I've been gambling for a while and I can't control it anymore. I'd like to come along. What happens at a meeting?"

When you post on a forum for the first time: "I'm new here. I mostly bet on [sports, casino, whatever it is] and I want to stop. I don't really know where to start." You don't have to share amounts or details.

When you call a helpline: "I think I have a gambling problem and I don't know what to do." That's enough. They'll ask the questions.

A few things to remember:

  • You can stay anonymous. You don't have to give your surname at a meeting or on a helpline. On a forum, use a name that can't be traced back to you.
  • Don't share personal details. No phone number, address or bank details on a forum. Not even if someone offers to help with money.
  • You can leave. If a group or forum doesn't suit you, try something else. That isn't failure.
  • Not sure it's bad enough? That's exactly the question most people start with. You don't have to wait until everything falls apart.

Group, forum or professional help: what fits you?

There's no right or wrong choice. A few questions that help:

  • Do you mostly want to tell your story and hear you're not the only one? A support group or a moderated forum fits.
  • Do you first want to talk to someone anonymously, without an appointment? Start with the National Gambling Helpline or GamCare's live chat.
  • Are you still gambling daily, deep in debt or feeling very low? Then professional help makes sense, alongside a group if you like. See where to get help for gambling addiction.

Many people combine them. A group for recognition, professional help for the approach, and someone close to you who knows.

Support for family and friends

Are you the partner, parent, child or friend of someone who gambles? You're affected too, and you don't have to carry it alone either.

  • Gam-Anon runs meetings for family and friends.
  • GamCare has monthly chatrooms for family and friends, and one-to-one support for people affected by someone else's gambling.
  • The National Gambling Helpline is there for you as well.

Frequently asked questions

Are there free gambling support groups near me?

In the UK, Gamblers Anonymous holds meetings every day of the week across England, Wales and Ulster, and you can just turn up. Use the meeting finder on gamblersanonymous.org.uk. In the US, search for Gamblers Anonymous with your city or state.

Is there an online forum for gambling addiction?

Yes. GamCare runs a moderated forum where you post under a username, plus daily online chatrooms. All free.

Do I have to talk at my first meeting?

No. You don't have to share anything you're not ready to share. If you're unsure, ask the group beforehand how a meeting works.

Can family members go to a support group?

Yes. Gam-Anon is for partners, family and friends, and GamCare has chatrooms and support for family members.

Is a support group enough to stop gambling?

For some people it is. Many do better with a group alongside other help. If you're still gambling a lot or have serious debt, get professional help too.
