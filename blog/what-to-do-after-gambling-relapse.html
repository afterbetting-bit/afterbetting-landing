Relapse

What to do right after a gambling relapse

The next hour, the next day and the next week. Not how bad you should feel.

By Afterbetting · updated 11 October 2026 · 8 min read

You placed a bet. Maybe after a week, maybe after six months. The money is gone, the shame is here, and you are reading this at a moment when you would rather be anywhere else.

Stop for a second. Breathe in. Breathe out.

What your head is telling you right now, that it was all for nothing, that you will never manage this, is not the truth. It is the addiction talking, as loud as it can, because it wants you to give up.

This page is about the next hour, the next day and the next week. Not about how bad you should feel.

You are not back at zero

A relapse is painful and it matters. But it does not erase the days you did not gamble. Those days happened. You learned things in them that you still know today.

Your streak resets. Your ability does not. Neither does your direction, or the person you were becoming in the meantime.

I gambled for more than eight years. I know the morning after. I know the feeling of having let everyone down again, yourself most of all.

From the founder: The shame after a relapse was the worst part. Not the money. The feeling that I had failed again. What helped was treating it like a data point rather than a verdict. What happened? What triggered it? What will I do differently? Those three questions replaced the spiral.

That is what this page is built on. A relapse is information, not a verdict.

What usually happens next

The hours after a relapse tend to follow the same pattern. Knowing it helps, because each stage has its own risk.

First the shame. Fast, sometimes within minutes. Shame makes you want to hide, to lie, to make it disappear.

Then the bargaining. "The money is gone anyway. I may as well carry on this weekend. I will start again on Monday." This is exactly how one bad evening turns into a lost month.

Then the numbness. A flat, empty feeling. Nothing seems worth the effort. This is where people disappear from recovery for weeks.

You do not have to fight all three today. You only have to do the next thing.

In the first hour: stop

Not tomorrow. Not after one more. Now.

  • Close the app. Log out of the site. Leave the bookmaker if you are in one.
  • Put your phone in another room for a while.
  • Do not try to win it back. The urge to chase is at its strongest right now, and chasing is what turns one loss into several.

You do not need to work out what happened yet. You do not need to add up the damage. Just stop.

In the first day: close the gap and tell one person

Close the gap you went through. Something let you in. Find it and shut it today.

  • Deleted the blocker? Reinstall it. BetBlocker is free. Gamban works on all your devices.
  • Turned off your bank's gambling block? Turn it back on. Most UK banks make you wait 48 hours or more to switch it off again, and that delay is exactly what you want.
  • Found a site that is not on GamStop? Read Found a casino not on GamStop? It is about this exact situation.

More on every lock in How to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments.

Tell one person. A partner, a friend, a parent, a counsellor. One message is enough: "I gambled again today. I want to talk about it."

Shame lives on secrecy. One honest conversation takes away a lot of its power. Not forever. But for today.

If there is nobody you can tell right now, call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. It is free and open 24/7.

In the first week: look at what happened

Not to punish yourself. To learn.

Take a piece of paper, or the journal in the app, and answer these questions honestly:

  1. What happened that day, before I gambled?
  2. What was I feeling?
  3. What did I tell myself just before?
  4. What was the moment I could have done something else?
  5. What will I do differently next time that moment comes?

Maybe it was stress at work. A row at home. A lonely Saturday. A match you told yourself you would only watch. An email from a bookmaker. Whatever it was, you know it now. Next time, you will recognise it earlier.

Then add one protection you did not have before. One extra lock, one extra person who knows, one evening a week that is filled before it starts. If the hours that went wrong were evenings or weekends, What to do instead of gambling helps you plan them.

Start again from day one

Your streak goes back to zero. That is not a punishment. It is a fact.

But this day one is not the same as your first day one. You know more than you did then. You have already shown you can go days, weeks or months without gambling. And you are getting back up after a relapse. That says something about you.

In the app you set a new start date in a few taps. Your journal, your habits and your money overview stay exactly as they were.

Telling your partner or family

If they knew you had stopped, this conversation is harder. A few things help.

Tell them yourself. Do not wait for them to find out. Being caught a second time does more damage than telling them. Try to do it within a day.

Do not make it smaller than it was. No "it was only once", no "it was less than before". Call it what it is.

Let them be angry or sad. They believed you. Their reaction is not an overreaction. It is real hurt.

Do not promise what you cannot keep. Not "it will never happen again". Instead: "I have reinstalled the blocker, I have turned my bank block back on and I am calling the helpline tomorrow."

Offer openness. If they want to, let them see your bank statements for a while, or your streak in the app. Trust comes back through what they can see, not through promises.

If the conversation feels impossible, How to tell someone you have a gambling problem walks you through it.

When to get more help

A relapse can be a sign that what worked before is not enough for what is going on now. That is not a failure. It is a reason to add something.

  • Call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. They can talk it through and tell you what support is available near you.
  • In England you can refer yourself to an NHS gambling clinic, or ask your GP about services in your area.
  • Already seeing someone? Go back and tell them what happened. No judgement. Just work.

Getting back up, today

The days and weeks you did not gamble were real. One relapse does not undo them.

Close the gap. Tell one person. Write down what happened. Set a new start date.

One thing at a time. Today.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do right after a gambling relapse?

Stop now, not after one more bet. Close the app or site, put your phone away and do not try to win it back. Then close the gap you went through, such as a removed blocker or bank block, and tell one person what happened.

Does a relapse mean I have failed?

No. Your streak resets, but the days you did not gamble still happened and what you learned in them stays. Treat the relapse as information: what triggered it, and what you will do differently next time.

Who can I call after a gambling relapse?

In the UK, the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133 is free and open 24/7. In England you can also refer yourself to an NHS gambling clinic, or ask your GP about support in your area.
