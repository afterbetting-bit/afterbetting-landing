Gambling over Christmas: how to get through the holidays

Extra money, days off, family and sometimes loneliness. How to plan the hardest weeks of the year.

By Afterbetting · 21 November 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

Christmas is one of the hardest times to stay away from gambling. Extra money, days off without a routine, drink, family, obligations and sometimes loneliness all come together, and the football keeps going. What helps: plan the hard days in advance, move any bonus straight out of reach, block before December starts, and agree with one person that you can call. More in how to stop gambling.

For most people December is the cosy month. For me it was one of the hardest of the year.

I gambled for eight years, mostly on sports, and ran up more than €40,000 in debt. The holidays were a trigger. Family, obligations and sometimes loneliness left me feeling off. And on exactly those days there was time, money and always a match on.

If this is your first or second Christmas without gambling, this is for you.

Why Christmas is so hard

It is several things at once.

  • Extra money. A Christmas bonus, the December payday that comes early, cash from family. A big amount in your account makes a bet feel small.
  • Days off with no routine. Your normal week disappears, and the routine was what held you up.
  • Family and obligations. Old roles, questions about work and money, tension at the table. Or the feeling you have to pretend everything is fine.
  • Loneliness. Not everyone has a full table. And you can feel alone in a full room too.
  • Drink. More parties, more dinners, more drinking. After a few drinks the next bet feels smaller than it is.
  • Football. Boxing Day and the fixtures around New Year keep going while you are off work.

If that sounds familiar, it is not strange that this time feels heavier. It is a predictable risk. And what is predictable can be planned.

Plan the hard days now

Look at the calendar today. Which days are the hardest? For many people it is Christmas Eve, Boxing Day and New Year's Eve, plus the empty days in between.

For each hard day, write down:

  • Where will you be and with whom?
  • What will you do in the hours you would normally bet?
  • Who can you text when the urge comes?
  • How do you leave if it gets too much?

It sounds clinical. But the difference between a plan and no plan shows up in the moment.

Move the extra money before it lands

Do not wait until the money is in your account.

  • Set up a transfer for the day your bonus or December pay arrives, into a savings account you cannot reach quickly.
  • Or use it straight away for a debt. Every pound that leaves your current account is one you cannot gamble.
  • Let someone you trust keep an eye on it if you can. If that feels like too much, the savings account alone is a start.

Block before December starts

Do it now, not on the night things go wrong.

  • Sign up to GamStop, or check your exclusion is still active.
  • Install a blocker on your phone and laptop.
  • Turn on the gambling block in your banking app.
  • Delete betting apps and turn off score apps that show odds.

Family, obligations and loneliness

You do not have to go to everything. A shorter evening still counts. You can leave early. You can skip a day.

If your family knows you have stopped, agree with one person that you can step outside if it gets too much. If they do not know, pick someone who does and who you can text that evening.

Spending Christmas alone? Then plan something with people on purpose. A walk, a gym that is open, volunteering, a support group. See gambling addiction support groups for what is out there.

Scratch cards, lotteries and card games

Over Christmas, gambling turns up in places you do not expect. A scratch card in a Christmas card. A ticket for a big New Year draw. A game of cards for money after dinner.

For someone who is quitting, that is not harmless. Ask family in advance not to give you scratch cards or lottery tickets. Skip games for money, even "just for fun". You do not have to explain. "I don't play for money" is enough.

A plan for the day itself

An example of what a hard day could look like:

  • Morning: exercise or a walk, before the day fills up.
  • Afternoon: time with people, phone in your coat, not in your hand.
  • Evening: agree a time to leave, and stick to it.
  • Afterwards: text your person that you are home. Short, just "home, went fine".

It sounds over the top. But a day with a shape is easier to get through than an empty day you have to sit out.

If it goes wrong anyway

Stop with the loss. Do not try to win it back, not even "one more because the day is ruined anyway". That is how one night becomes a week.

Tell one person. Block whatever is still open. And read what to do after a gambling relapse. A relapse does not send you back to zero. You know more than you did last time.

After the holidays

January can be hard too. The money is gone, the days are short and the routine has to come back. Plan the first week of January as carefully as Christmas itself.

What helped me, in December and the rest of the year, was a fixed routine for the hours I used to gamble, sport, and a few months of professional help. It did not work all at once. But every Christmas without gambling made the next one easier.

Help over Christmas

  • UK: National Gambling Helpline 0808 8020 133, free, 24/7.
  • US: 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), call or text, 24/7.
  • Thinking about suicide? In the UK call Samaritans on 116 123.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I want to gamble more at Christmas?

Because a lot comes together: extra money, days off without a routine, drink, family, obligations and sometimes loneliness, plus a busy football schedule. It is a predictable risk, which means you can plan for it.

What should I do with my Christmas bonus if I am quitting gambling?

Set up a transfer so it goes straight into a savings account you cannot reach quickly, or use it for a debt on the day it arrives.

How do I get through Christmas alone without gambling?

Plan time with people for the hardest hours: a walk, sport, volunteering or a support group. Agree with someone that you can text them.

I gambled over Christmas. What now?

Stop with the loss, do not chase it, tell one person and block whatever is still open. The National Gambling Helpline is open 24/7 on 0808 8020 133.
