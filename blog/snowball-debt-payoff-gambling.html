Snowball or avalanche: which way to pay off gambling debt?

Smallest debt first, or highest interest first. One example, both ways.

By Afterbetting · updated 11 October 2026 · 8 min read

You have written down your debts. You know the number. Now the question is: which one do you pay off first?

There are two well-known ways to do it. The snowball: smallest debt first. The avalanche: highest interest first. Both work. Both are better than spreading a little extra across everything. This page explains both, works through one example, and helps you choose.

The basics, for both methods

Both methods start the same way:

  1. Keep paying the minimum on every debt. Missing payments makes everything worse.
  2. Put essentials first: rent, council tax, energy. See How to rebuild your finances after gambling for that order.
  3. Decide how much you can pay in total each month.
  4. Everything above the minimums goes to one debt at a time.
  5. When that debt is gone, its whole payment moves to the next one.

That last step is where the name comes from. The payment rolls on and grows, like a snowball.

The only difference between the two methods is which debt you aim at first.

The snowball: smallest debt first

You put all the extra money on the debt with the smallest balance, whatever its interest rate. When it is cleared, you move to the next smallest.

Why it helps: you see a whole debt disappear early. Then another. For many people that is what keeps them going through the long months of repaying.

The cost: if a bigger debt has a high interest rate, you may pay a bit more interest in total.

The avalanche: highest interest first

You put all the extra money on the debt with the highest interest rate, whatever its size. When it is cleared, you move to the next highest rate.

Why it helps: you pay the least interest overall, and you are often debt-free a little sooner.

The cost: if the most expensive debt is also a big one, it can take many months before you clear your first debt. That can feel like nothing is happening.

One example, both ways

Say you owe this, and you can pay £400 a month in total:

  • Credit card: £2,400 at 24% interest a year, minimum £70
  • Overdraft: £800 at about 40% a year, minimum £30
  • Loan from your brother: £1,500, no interest, £50 a month
  • Personal loan: £4,000 at 9% a year, minimum £120

The minimums add up to £270. So there is £130 a month extra to aim at one debt.

With the snowball (smallest first: overdraft, brother, credit card, personal loan):

  • Overdraft gone after 6 months
  • Your brother paid back after 12 months
  • Credit card gone after 20 months
  • Debt-free after 26 months, about £1,320 in interest

With the avalanche (highest rate first: overdraft, credit card, personal loan, brother):

  • Overdraft gone after 6 months
  • Credit card gone after 17 months
  • Personal loan gone after 24 months
  • Debt-free after 25 months, about £1,120 in interest

In this example the difference is one month and about £200. That is typical: the avalanche saves some money, but often less than people expect. The snowball gives you two cleared debts in the first year instead of one, and in this case your brother is paid back more than a year earlier.

These numbers are an example to show how the methods work. Your own figures will be different.

Which one should you choose?

Choose the snowball if:

  • you have tried to pay off debt before and gave up,
  • you have several small debts that you can clear within months,
  • you owe money to family or friends and want to clear those first,
  • you need to see progress to keep going.

Choose the avalanche if:

  • one debt has a much higher rate than the rest, like an overdraft or a store card,
  • you can stick to a plan without needing early wins,
  • the debts are of similar size anyway.

You can also mix them. Many people clear one or two small debts first for the boost, and then switch to the highest rate. That is fine. A plan you keep to beats the perfect plan you give up on.

What it was like for me

I had more than 40,000 in debt after more than eight years of gambling. What I learned is that the method matters less than two other things: that the gambling has stopped, and that you can see the progress.

From the founder: Facing the real number was one of the hardest and most important things I did. Once it was written down, it stopped being a shapeless dread and became a maths problem. Maths problems have solutions. Shapeless dread does not.

Mistakes that undo either method

Gambling to speed it up. The thought that one good win would clear the lot is the most dangerous one. If I had won it back, I would have gambled it again.

Borrowing more to pay faster. A new loan while the gambling is recent often ends up as an extra debt.

Stopping the minimums on other debts. Both methods only work if every other debt stays up to date.

Doing it alone when it is too much. If your minimum payments are more than you can afford, get free debt advice first. StepChange and National Debtline (0808 808 4000) are free. In England and Wales they can also apply for Breathing Space for you.

Frequently asked questions

Is the snowball or avalanche method better for gambling debt?

Both work. The avalanche usually saves some interest. The snowball clears whole debts sooner, which helps many people keep going. In our example the difference was one month and about £200. Pick the one you will stick to.

What is the debt snowball method?

You pay the minimum on every debt and put all extra money on the smallest balance. When it is cleared, that whole payment moves to the next smallest debt, so the payment grows as you go.

What is the debt avalanche method?

You pay the minimum on every debt and put all extra money on the debt with the highest interest rate. When it is cleared, you move to the next highest rate. It usually costs the least interest overall.
