Is Kalshi gambling? An honest answer from a former bettor

Kalshi and Polymarket call it trading. If you are trying to quit sports betting, here is what that means for you.

By Afterbetting · 21 November 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

If you put money on the outcome of a match, an election or anything else and win or lose depending on what happens, it works like gambling, whatever the app calls it. For someone trying to quit sports betting, Kalshi and Polymarket carry the same risk as a sportsbook. A sportsbook self-exclusion does not cover them, so exclude there too and block them. More in how to stop sports betting.

If you are asking whether Kalshi is gambling, there is a good chance you already have a feeling about the answer. Maybe you self-excluded from the sportsbooks and found that this app still works. Maybe you started with a contract on an election and ended up on Sunday football. Maybe someone told you it is trading, not betting, and part of you wanted that to be true.

I bet on sports for eight years. It started at 16 and it ended with more than €40,000 in debt. I am not a lawyer and this is not about what regulators call it. This is about what it does to someone who struggles to stop.

What Kalshi and Polymarket actually are

Kalshi and Polymarket are prediction markets. You buy a "contract" on whether something will happen. Will this team win tonight. Will this candidate win. Will it rain in New York on Friday. If it happens, the contract pays out. If it does not, you lose what you paid.

Prices move as other people buy and sell, so it looks like a trading app. There are charts, order books and positions. Kalshi is regulated as an exchange in the US, not as a sportsbook, and the companies avoid the word betting.

But look at what you are doing. You put money on an uncertain outcome, you wait, and you win or lose depending on what happens. That is the same loop.

Why the label does not matter if you are trying to quit

A gambling problem is not about the license on the app. It is about the pull. The checking. The next game. The feeling that you almost had it. The money that should have gone to something else.

If you bet on sports with a sportsbook, you know that loop. Buying a contract on the same match runs the same loop with a different name. Your brain does not care whether the screen says "bet" or "position".

And the trading look can make it worse. When I was betting, I told myself my knowledge of the sport made it skill. Charts and order books give you an even better story: you are not gambling, you are reading the market.

Why it pulls so hard on former sports bettors

Prediction markets fit a sports bettor's habits almost perfectly.

  • Every game is there. The same leagues, the same matches, the same Sunday afternoons.
  • It feels smarter. You are not backing a team, you are "buying at 40 cents". For someone who already believed their knowledge made it skill, that is a strong pull.
  • Small amounts. A contract can cost less than a coffee. Small amounts are how it started for a lot of us.
  • It is always open. There is always another market: the next game, the next quarter, the weather, the news.
  • Younger users. In many states you can use these apps at 18, while sportsbooks usually require 21.

None of that makes you weak for being drawn in. It makes it a product to treat with the same caution as a sportsbook.

The self-exclusion gap

This is the part that catches people.

If you self-excluded from your sportsbooks, through the operators or your state's program, that exclusion covers those sportsbooks. It does not automatically cover prediction markets. They are a different kind of company under different rules.

So someone can do everything right, exclude from every sportsbook, and then find that betting on the same games is still one download away. US news outlets have reported on people in recovery who relapsed exactly that way in 2026.

If that happened to you, it is not proof you are weak. It is a gap in the system. Close it.

How to self-exclude from Kalshi and Polymarket

Both platforms have their own tools. Check their help pages for the current steps, because these change.

  • Kalshi: under its responsible trading options you can take a break, set a limit on what you deposit, or self-exclude. Kalshi says self-exclusion does not cover access through some brokers that connect to its exchange, so also block those.
  • Polymarket: offers self-exclusion for 30 days, one year or permanently, and deposit limits.
  • SelfExclude.io: a service that lets you exclude from several prediction market and crypto betting platforms in one go.

Pick the longest option you can stand. A short break ends at exactly the moment you are most likely to go back.

Block the apps, not just the accounts

Self-exclusion closes the account. A blocker closes the door.

  • Install a blocker such as Gamban. It also blocks prediction market platforms.
  • Delete the apps and turn off notifications before you delete them, so you do not get pulled back by one alert.
  • Turn on the gambling block in your banking app if your bank has one. Check your statements afterwards. If payments to these platforms still go through, ask your bank to block them.
  • Let someone you trust hold any codes or passwords.

Signs it has become a problem

  • You check prices during work, at dinner or in the middle of the night.
  • You trade on sports you do not follow, just to have something running.
  • You put money back in right after a loss to win it back.
  • You hide how much you put in from your partner or family.
  • You already self-excluded from sportsbooks and moved here instead.
  • You feel restless or irritable when you have no open position.

If several of these fit, the gambling addiction test takes two minutes and is anonymous.

If you already relapsed on a prediction market

Stop with the loss. Do not try to win it back first. Chasing losses is how one bad night becomes a bad month.

Then do three things today. Self-exclude on the platform. Install a blocker. Tell one person. If you want a plan for the next 24 hours, read what to do after a gambling relapse.

What actually helped me

Blocking bought me time. What I did with that time mattered more. The betting sat in my evenings and weekends, often with a drink. Those hours needed something else in them: a routine, sport, and a few months of professional help. I paid my debt back in almost two years.

Where to get help in the US

Call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), the National Problem Gambling Helpline. It is free, confidential and open 24/7. You do not have to be in crisis to call.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kalshi gambling or investing?

Legally it is regulated as an exchange in the US. In practice, if you put money on whether an event happens and win or lose on the outcome, it works the same way as a bet and can become a problem the same way.

Does sportsbook self-exclusion cover Kalshi?

No. Self-exclusion from sportsbooks or a state program does not automatically cover prediction markets. You need to self-exclude on Kalshi and Polymarket separately and use a blocker.

Can you get addicted to prediction markets?

Yes. The loop is the same as sports betting: money on an uncertain outcome, waiting, winning or losing, and the urge to go again. People in recovery from sports betting are especially at risk.

How do I block Kalshi and Polymarket?

Self-exclude in each app, install a blocker such as Gamban that covers prediction markets, delete the apps, and turn on a gambling block at your bank if it has one.

Is Polymarket gambling?

The same answer as Kalshi. If you put money on outcomes, it works like betting. Polymarket offers self-exclusion for 30 days, one year or permanently.
