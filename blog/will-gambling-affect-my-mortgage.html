Will gambling affect my mortgage application?

What lenders see, how long it lasts, and what you can do while you wait.

By Afterbetting · 20 October 2026 · 8 min read

I had more than €40,000 of gambling debt. I paid it off in almost two years. I have been gambling-free for more than two years now.

And I still cannot get a mortgage. Because of my credit record.

I am not telling you this to scare you. I am telling you because nobody told me in advance. Stopping is one thing. The consequences run on for a while. It helps to know how that works, so you can plan for it.

I live in the Netherlands, where the credit register works differently from the UK. This article explains how it works in the UK, based on what lenders and credit agencies say.

Will gambling show on my credit file?

No. Gambling itself is not on your credit file. What is on it is what the gambling left behind. The loan you took out to win it back. The credit card you maxed out. The overdraft you lived in. Missed payments, defaults and County Court Judgments.

A default stays on your UK credit file for six years from the date it was registered. Even if you pay it off. Paying it off does change the status to settled, and lenders do look at that.

Where gambling does show: your bank statements

This is the part many people do not expect. When you apply for a mortgage, lenders usually ask for your recent bank statements. And there, gambling is visible, line by line.

Brokers say lenders look for patterns more than single bets. Things that raise questions:

  • Frequent gambling transactions, for example several deposits a week
  • Large amounts compared with your income
  • A pattern of losses followed by repeated top-ups
  • Gambling alongside an overdraft, missed payments or payday loans
  • Gambling spend going up in the months before you apply

An occasional small bet with an account in credit is usually not a problem. A history like mine would be.

What you can do now

1. Check your credit file. You can see your statutory credit report for free from Experian, Equifax and TransUnion. Do it before you speak to a broker, so nothing in that conversation surprises you.

2. Fix what is wrong. If something is incorrect, like a default that should not be there or a debt shown as open that you settled long ago, contact the lender who reported it. You can also raise a dispute with the credit agency.

3. Keep your statements clean. Turn on the gambling block in your banking app and leave it on. Every month without gambling transactions is a month that counts in your favour. Most lenders look at the last three to six months.

4. Deal with the debt that is left. Pick a method you can stick to. How to pay off gambling debt and rebuild your finances has a step-by-step plan, and Snowball or avalanche? helps you choose an order.

5. Talk to a broker, and be honest. A good broker knows which lenders look at past problems differently, and when. That depends on your situation.

6. Use the time. While you wait, save. A deposit and a buffer make a difference later. They also show, on paper and to yourself, that things are different now.

Be careful with "credit repair"

You will find companies that promise to clean up your credit file for a fee. Be very careful. Accurate information stays until its time is up. If something is wrong, you can dispute it yourself, for free.

I get the temptation. After years of debt you want it to be over. But this is paying for a quick fix. You know that pattern.

How I look at it

It is frustrating. I paid it all back and I am still held back by it.

You can also look at it like this: the record is about the past. Your statements today show a different person. One day that record is gone. Your recovery stays.

A mortgage is a goal you can work towards. That is different from a door that is shut for good.

Still in debt?

Frequently asked questions

Does gambling show on your credit file?

No. Gambling itself is not recorded. Loans, credit cards, overdrafts, missed payments and defaults are. If you used them because of gambling, that is what shows.

Can I get a mortgage if I gamble?

Occasional small bets with an account in credit are usually not a problem. Frequent gambling, large amounts or gambling alongside money trouble on your bank statements can lead to questions or a refusal.

How many months of bank statements do mortgage lenders check?

Usually around three to six months, but it varies by lender. Keep gambling transactions off your statements well before you apply.

How long does a default stay on my credit file in the UK?

Six years from the date it was registered, even if you pay it off. Settling it does change its status, which lenders take into account.
