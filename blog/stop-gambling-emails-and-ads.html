How to stop gambling emails, texts and ads

You stopped, but the bonus emails keep coming. How to stop as much of it as you can.

By Afterbetting · 21 November 2026 · 5 min read

The short answer

Stop gambling marketing in layers: unsubscribe from every operator and switch off marketing in your account settings, object to direct marketing under data protection law if they keep going, block senders and numbers, forward spam texts to 7726, and turn off gambling ads in Google My Ad Center. Self-exclusion helps, but check your settings anyway. More in block gambling sites and payments.

You stopped. You signed up to GamStop, you installed a blocker. And then an email arrives with a bonus. Or a text on a Friday night: "We miss you, here are 50 free spins."

I got a lot of emails and texts, also after I had stopped. What I did: block them, delete them and unsubscribe from every newsletter. That sounds small. But every message is a nudge at a moment you did not choose.

Here is how to stop as much of it as you can.

Why it matters

When you are trying to stop, an ad is more than an ad. A bonus gives you a reason you did not have to come up with yourself. "It is free money." "Just once, it is already in the account." That is how a lot of relapses start.

The fewer nudges you get, the fewer moments you have to resist.

Step 1: unsubscribe from every operator

  • Click "unsubscribe" at the bottom of every email. Do it for every company you ever had an account with.
  • Log in and switch off all marketing in your account settings: email, text, phone and push notifications.
  • Reply STOP to texts where that option is given.

In Great Britain, licensed operators have needed your opt-in for direct marketing since May 2025, per product and per channel, with the boxes unticked by default. If you never opted in and they still message you, that is worth a complaint.

Step 2: check after self-exclusion

Self-exclusion through GamStop or with an operator should stop marketing from licensed operators in Great Britain. Operators are also expected to keep self-excluded customers out of paid ads where they reasonably can. But companies make mistakes, and offshore sites do not follow these rules at all.

So unsubscribe yourself anyway, and keep a note of anything that still arrives.

Step 3: object if they keep going

If an operator keeps emailing or texting, you have a right under data protection law to object to direct marketing. For direct marketing, the company has to stop.

Send a short email to customer services or the operator's data protection address:

Example: I object to the use of my personal data for direct marketing. Please stop sending me marketing by email, text, phone or push notification, and confirm this has been done.

Keep a copy. If they carry on, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) in the UK, or tell the Gambling Commission.

Step 4: block senders and forward spam texts

  • Add gambling senders to your email block list, or set a filter that deletes them straight away.
  • Block the numbers texts come from.
  • In the UK and the US, forward spam texts to 7726. Your network uses it to track and stop spam.
  • Consider a fresh email address for the rest of your life and let the old one fade out. Some people find that is the only thing that brings real quiet.

Step 5: turn off gambling ads on Google and YouTube

Google lets you limit ads about sensitive topics, and gambling is one of them.

  1. Go to My Ad Center (myadcenter.google.com) and sign in.
  2. Choose Customise ads, then the Sensitive tab.
  3. Switch off Gambling.

It applies to Search, YouTube and Discover while you are signed in. Google says you may still see the odd ad, for example when you search for something gambling-related yourself.

Step 6: Instagram, Facebook and TikTok

Social media is harder. Ads for unlicensed casinos turn up too, sometimes aimed at exactly the people who have excluded.

  • Tap the three dots on every gambling ad and hide or report it.
  • Check the app's ad preferences to see less of certain topics.
  • Unfollow tipsters, gambling streamers and "free bonus" pages.

An ad that promises casinos "not on GamStop" is an unlicensed operator. Do not click. If you already did, read found a casino not on GamStop.

Step 7: take the triggers off your phone

  • Delete betting apps and sports apps that show odds.
  • Turn off notifications from anything that pulls you toward the matches you used to bet on.
  • Install a blocker, so a tap on an ad leads nowhere.

When a bonus slips through anyway

At some point an email will get through. Decide now what you will do then:

  1. Do not open it. Delete it from your inbox.
  2. Block the sender straight away, so it does not happen again.
  3. Feel the urge? Set a 15-minute timer and do something else until it ends. Urges rise, peak and fade.
  4. Text someone who knows. Just: "Got a gambling email, I'm fine."

A bonus feels like free money. That is exactly why it exists.

Ads around sport

Betting brands are still everywhere around sport: shirts, pitch-side boards, ad breaks, pundit segments. Since the 2026/27 season, Premier League clubs no longer carry gambling sponsors on the front of their shirts, but sleeves, boards and broadcasts are a different story. If the pre-match and half-time segments trigger you, skip them and mute the ad breaks.

It does not have to be perfect

You will not get rid of everything. An ad will slip through. The goal is not zero. The goal is fewer, so the moments that are left are ones you can handle.

Help

  • UK: National Gambling Helpline 0808 8020 133, free, 24/7.
  • US: 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), call or text, 24/7.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop gambling emails?

Unsubscribe at the bottom of each email, switch off marketing in your account settings, and if they continue, object to direct marketing in writing. Then block the sender.

Does GamStop stop gambling marketing?

It should stop marketing from operators licensed in Great Britain, but unsubscribe yourself anyway. Offshore sites do not follow the rules.

How do I stop gambling texts?

Reply STOP where offered, switch off text marketing in your account, block the number and forward spam texts to 7726 in the UK or US.

Can I turn off gambling ads on YouTube?

Yes. In Google My Ad Center choose Customise ads, the Sensitive tab, and switch off Gambling. It works while you are signed in.
