Can you cancel GamStop early? Read this first

The GamStop rules, explained honestly. And what you can do if the urge to gamble is strong right now.

By Afterbetting · 11 October 2026 · 8 min read

You are here because you want to end your GamStop exclusion. Or because you want to know if you could.

Both are fine. I am not going to lecture you.

I will give you the rules first, exactly as they are. Then I will tell you what I think is going on in your head right now. Because I know that thought well. I gambled for eight years. I know how convincing a plan can sound when it is really the urge doing the talking.

The short answer: the rules

These come from GamStop itself.

  • You chose a minimum period. Six months, one year, five years, or five years that renews automatically.
  • Nobody can end it early. Not you, not GamStop, not a support agent on a good day. Their own words: they cannot remove anyone's exclusion early.
  • It does not end by itself. When your minimum period is over, the exclusion stays in place. If you do nothing, it carries on for another seven years.
  • To remove it after it has expired, you have to ask. You check your account, request removal, and GamStop processes it.
  • Then there is a 24-hour cooling-off period. During those 24 hours you can still cancel the removal in your account.

So if you are still inside your minimum period, the answer is simple. It cannot be done.

Keep reading anyway. Especially then.

Why the rules are this strict

They can feel unfair. It is your life, your money, your choice.

But think back to the day you signed up. You knew something then that is easy to forget now: that moments like this would come. Moments when you would want back in. And that on those moments, you could not fully trust yourself.

That is not weakness. It is how addiction works. The part of your brain that wants to gamble is faster and louder than the part that thinks things through. That is why the minimum period exists. That is why there is a cooling-off period. They do not protect the person you are tonight. They protect the person who made the decision on a clear day.

You closed a door on purpose. For a reason.

What is going on in your head

The urge rarely shows up as an urge. It shows up as a plan. A reasonable-sounding plan.

Do any of these sound familiar?

"I have it under control now."
Things are better. Months without a bet. So surely a little is fine. But you have it under control precisely because you are not gambling. That is the exact thing that changes the moment you start again.

"Just a small amount. Just for fun."
Mine never started big. It started with a small bet on a bored evening. The big amounts came later. That is the pattern, not the exception.

"I need to win it back."
This is the dangerous one. You look at your debt and see one way out: one good night. I thought exactly this with more than €40,000 of debt. Trying to win it back is how the debt got that big.

"Everything is so bad, it doesn't matter anymore."
That is not a gambling thought. That is a sign things are genuinely hard right now. I will come back to this at the end.

If one of these hit home, you do not have to decide anything. You only have to see it for what it is.

Three questions before you do anything

If your minimum period is over and you are thinking about removal, ask yourself these three questions. Honestly. Write the answers down if you can.

1. Why now?
What happened this week? Payday, an argument, boredom, a big match, an advert, a bad night's sleep? The urge almost always follows a trigger. If you can point to one, this is probably not a calm decision.

2. Who knows about this?
Would you tell your partner, a friend or your family that you are removing GamStop? If the answer is no, you already know most of what you need to know. What we hide already feels like a relapse on the inside.

3. What does it look like a month from now?
Play the film to the end. Not to the first win, but to the night after. And the week after. Where are you? What is in your account? Who are you explaining things to?

Some people read these questions and still want to go ahead. That is your call. At least it will be a conscious one.

If you do request removal: use the 24 hours

The cooling-off period is short. Use every hour of it.

  • Tell one person. Not to ask permission. Just so it is not happening in secret.
  • Put your money somewhere safe. Bills to a separate account. A fixed weekly amount to live on. Money that is not there cannot be gambled.
  • Check in with yourself before the 24 hours are up. Is the reason from yesterday still there? Urges often fade within a day or two. You can cancel the removal in your account, and that is not failing. That is exactly what the cooling-off period is for.

If your period has ended without you noticing

Because GamStop stays in place until you ask, nothing changes on its own. That is a good thing. If you want to stay protected, you do not need to do anything.

If you are close to the end of a six-month or one-year period and you want certainty, the five-year option with auto-renewal exists for a reason. Choosing too long is better than too short. Removal is always possible later. Getting back in after a relapse costs far more.

What GamStop does and does not cover

GamStop is strong, but it is not complete. It covers websites and apps run by companies licensed in Great Britain.

It does not cover sites without a British licence. Those sites do not check GamStop, and many of them advertise exactly that. If you want to know how self-exclusion works in practice and where the gaps are, read Does self-exclusion work for gambling?

GamStop is one lock on the door. The more locks, the more time between the thought and the action. Blocking software on your phone and a gambling block on your bank card add two more. In that extra time, the urge often passes on its own.

If you are about to gamble right now

Maybe you are not reading this out of curiosity. Maybe the urge is strong right now.

Do this, in this order:

  1. Delay it by 15 minutes. Not forever. Fifteen minutes. Set a timer. Urges peak and then fade, usually within 20 minutes.
  2. Get up and go somewhere else. Another room, outside, up and down the stairs. Move your body and your head follows.
  3. Text someone. "I'm having a hard moment. Can you call me?" That is all you need to say.
  4. Call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. Free, 24/7.

What I want you to take with you

Your exclusion was your decision, made on a clear day. That decision deserves to be taken seriously. Especially by you.

You do not have to fix anything tonight. You only have to not gamble tonight. Tomorrow you look again.

Frequently asked questions

Can you cancel GamStop?

Not during your minimum period. Nobody can end it early. After that period, the exclusion stays in place until you ask GamStop to remove it, followed by a 24-hour cooling-off period.

How long does GamStop last?

You choose six months, one year or five years. When that period is over, the exclusion does not end by itself. If you do nothing, it carries on for another seven years.

What happens when GamStop ends?

Your minimum period ending does not lift the block. You have to log in and request removal. Then there is a 24-hour cooling-off period, and you can still cancel the removal in that time.

How does GamStop work?

You sign up once, and online gambling sites and apps licensed in Great Britain have to block you for the period you chose. Sites without a British licence do not check GamStop. More in does self-exclusion work?
