Quit gambling app: which ones actually help?

Blockers, self-exclusion and recovery apps: what each does, what it doesn't, and how to combine them.

By Afterbetting · 8 November 2026 · 6 min read

Your phone is where the gambling lives. It's also where you can stop it. So it makes sense that you're looking for an app to quit.

But "an app to stop gambling" can mean three very different things. An app that blocks gambling sites. A scheme that excludes you from licensed operators. And an app that helps you with the days themselves: the evenings, the urges, the money.

I gambled for 8 years, mostly on sports. When I stopped, I could find blockers, but nothing that helped in the hours that mattered. So I built Afterbetting. Below is an honest overview of what's out there, including what isn't mine, and how to combine it.

Type 1: blocking apps

A blocking app stops gambling sites and apps from opening on your phone or laptop. The best known are:

  • BetBlocker: free. Blocks gambling sites on your phone and computer.
  • Gamban: paid. Blocks gambling sites and apps, including across several devices.

Check their own websites for current prices and features.

What a blocker does well: it removes the quick route. On a bad night there's suddenly a barrier between the urge and the bet. Those few minutes can make the difference.

What it doesn't do: it doesn't take the urge away. And if you really want to, you can sometimes find a way around it. A blocker is a lock on the door, not a reason not to want to go in.

Type 2: self-exclusion and your bank

These aren't apps, but you can set them up in a few minutes on your phone.

  • GamStop (UK): sign up at gamstop.co.uk and you're excluded from all online gambling sites licensed in Great Britain, for 6 months, 1 year or 5 years. In the US there's no single national scheme. Self-exclusion runs per state.
  • A gambling block at your bank: many banks and card providers can block payments to gambling sites. You switch it on in your banking app or ask your bank.

Together with a blocking app, this makes it a lot harder to start. Does it actually work? The honest answer is in does self-exclusion work?.

Type 3: recovery apps

A recovery app doesn't block anything. It helps with what's left when the gambling is gone: the empty evenings, the urges, the debt, the feeling that you're not getting anywhere.

For me, this was the biggest gap. Blocking held me back for a while. But I almost never gambled during the day. It went wrong in the evenings and at weekends, the hours with nothing planned. No blocker fills those hours. What worked for me was rhythm: fixed things at fixed times, exercise on the nights I used to bet, and seeing my money again. More on that in why can't I stop gambling?.

A good recovery app helps with that. What should you look for?

  • Help with urges, one tap away. When it's going wrong, you don't want to search. The help has to be right there.
  • Seeing your days add up. Every day without gambling counts. Watching the number grow helps.
  • Honest about relapse. An app that drops you back to zero and says nothing else makes it worse. You deserve a way to start again without losing everything.
  • Your money in view. For a lot of people, money is the biggest damage. Seeing what you're no longer losing, and where you stand with your debt, brings calm.
  • Private. Your journal and your numbers are yours. Check where your data is stored and who can see it.
  • No gambling-style tricks. No spins, no prize draws, no rewards that feel like winning. That's what you're quitting.

How to combine them

It works best when you use all three. Each one does something different.

  1. Today: block. Install a blocking app and delete every gambling app. Ten minutes.
  2. Today or tomorrow: sign up for self-exclusion and switch on the gambling block at your bank.
  3. From today: fill your days. Use a recovery app for the urges, your routine and your money. Start with one thing: your start date, and one reason why you're stopping.

Blocking buys you time. What you do with that time decides whether it lasts.

Your first week with a recovery app

An app only helps if you use it in the moments that matter. Here's how to get the most out of the first week:

  • Day 1: set your start date and write down one reason. Not ten. One. The one that gets to you. You'll see it again when the urge comes.
  • Day 1: add roughly what went to gambling each week. An estimate is enough. From then on you see every day what you're no longer losing.
  • Day 2: pick your risky hours. Which evenings or moments did it go wrong? Put one fixed habit there. Exercise, a walk, calling someone.
  • Day 3: try Help now once, even without an urge. Then you know where it is when you need it. In the moment, you don't want to search.
  • Every evening: one minute. How was today? One thing that went well. That's enough.
  • Day 7: look back. How many days, how much money, which evening was hardest? Adjust your habits to that.

It doesn't have to be perfect. Skipping a day isn't a relapse. The point is to open the app at the moment you would otherwise have opened your betting app.

I didn't have this back then. I used the same methods, but without an overview in one place.

An app is a start, not treatment

An app can do a lot, but not everything. Are you still gambling every day, deep in debt, or feeling very low? Then talk to someone too. In the UK, call the National Gambling Helpline free on 0808 8020 133, 24/7. In the US, call 1-800-MY-RESET. If you're having thoughts of suicide, call emergency services or a crisis line right away.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an app to stop gambling?

Yes, there are three kinds. Blocking apps like BetBlocker (free) and Gamban (paid) stop gambling sites from opening. Self-exclusion like GamStop and a bank gambling block shut you out of licensed operators. Recovery apps like Afterbetting help with urges, your days and your money. They work best together.

Does a gambling blocker actually work?

A blocker makes it harder to gamble, and that helps in bad moments. It doesn't take the urge away. That's why it works best together with self-exclusion and something for the hours when you would otherwise gamble.

Is there a free app to stop gambling?

Yes. BetBlocker is free. Afterbetting's basics are free and stay free, no card needed. Plus costs 4.99 a month or 39.99 a year, and you can try it free for 7 days.

Can anyone else see what I write in the app?

No. Your journal is private, only you read it. Your data is encrypted in transit and at rest.

Can I use an app alongside treatment?

Yes. An app is for the hours between appointments: the evenings, the weekends, the moment the urge hits. It doesn't replace treatment.
