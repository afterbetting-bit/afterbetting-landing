How I paid off my gambling debt: from €40k to zero

Eight years of gambling, more than €40,000 owed, almost two years of paying it off. What I did and what I learned.

By the founder of Afterbetting · 17 October 2026 · 9 min read

A few months ago I made the last payment on my debt.

Do you know how much you have lost? Work it out with the gambling loss calculator. Anonymous, in your own browser.

No party. No announcement. I sat at my desk, looked at the balance and thought: done.

I gambled for more than eight years. By the end I owed more than €40,000. I was lying to everyone, myself included. Paying it off took almost two years.

This is how I did it. No trick, no system you have to buy. Just what I did, what was hard about it, and what I learned.

If you are sitting on gambling debt right now, I hope something here helps. Not to make you feel worse. To show you it can be done.

The night I wrote down the real number

For a long time I did not know exactly how much I owed. That was not an accident. I did not want to know.

A number in your head can shrink whenever you need it to. "A few grand." "It is not that bad." "I will win it back."

A number on paper cannot.

One evening I wrote everything down. Every loan, every credit card, every amount I owed to anyone. Then I added it up.

It was more than I thought. A lot more.

That moment was heavy. But it was also the first time I stopped lying to myself. From there I could actually do something. You cannot fix a number you refuse to look at.

If you do not know your exact total, that is your first step. Not paying. Not planning. Writing it down. I explain how to build that overview in how to pay off gambling debt.

Stop first, then pay

It sounds obvious. It is also the part I avoided for the longest.

While I was still gambling, the debt never felt like a real problem. In my head there was always a way out: one good win, and it would all be gone.

Then one day I saw it clearly. If I won it back, I would just gamble it again. Winning was never the way out. It was the reason I kept playing.

That one thought changed more than any promise I ever made to myself. I wrote more about it in how to stop gambling when you are winning.

Paying off only started once I had stopped. Every pound or euro you pay off while you are still gambling is money you can lose again tomorrow. That is not paying off debt. That is bailing water with the tap still running.

Living on a fixed amount

The core of how I did it is honestly boring.

I lived on a fixed amount each month. Enough for the bills and the real basics. Nothing more.

Everything above that went to the debt.

No budget with twenty categories. No app tracking every penny. One line: this is what I spend this month, the rest is not mine.

Why did that work? Because I did not have to decide every day. Deciding was exactly where things went wrong for me. Every decision is a chance to talk yourself into "just this once".

With a fixed amount, the decision was already made, at the start of the month. After that I only had to stick to it.

It sounds strict. It was. But it also gave me calm. I always knew where I stood.

Everything left over went to the debt

Money left at the end of the month? To the debt.

Something extra coming in? To the debt.

In those two years I never once thought "I have earned this, I can treat myself." Not because I had not earned it. Because I knew every euro I did not pay off made the whole thing take longer.

There are different ways to choose which debt to tackle first. Smallest first, so you feel progress. Or most expensive first, so you pay the least interest. I explain both in snowball or avalanche.

Which method you pick matters less than you think. What matters is that you keep going every month. Including the months you really do not feel like it.

Working hard with nothing to show for it

This was the hardest part for me. And nobody warned me about it.

You work hard. You stick to your number. You say no to things you would like to say yes to.

And you get nothing back. Not right away.

No new car. No holiday. No savings account growing. Just a number slowly getting smaller. And that number is still debt.

Gambling had taught me the exact opposite. The reward came instantly. Bet, result, feeling. A few seconds.

Paying off debt works the other way round. You put in the effort now, and the reward comes much later. Some days it feels like it never will.

I learned that this is okay. You do not need to get something back straight away. I knew why I was doing it. That was enough. Not every day, but whenever I stopped and thought about it properly.

That might be the biggest lesson of those two years. And not only about money.

Not being able to show anything

There was something else I had underestimated.

Around me, people showed how well they were doing. A new phone, a weekend away, a nice house. I worked just as hard, maybe harder. But I had nothing to show.

Because I had nothing, really. Everything I earned disappeared into a debt nobody could see.

That is hard sometimes. It is human to want people to see you are doing well. Especially after years of lying about how things really were.

I used to care about that a lot. I do not anymore.

What I learned: what other people show says nothing about what they have. And the thing I could not show was probably the most valuable thing I did in those years.

What I would tell you

I am not a debt adviser. I cannot tell you what is best in your situation. I can tell you what I would do if I had to start over.

  • Write down the real number. All of it. Including the debts you are most ashamed of. Including money owed to family or friends.
  • Stop gambling first. While you are still gambling, paying off is pointless. Use whatever helps: self-exclusion, blocking software, someone who keeps an eye on things with you.
  • Do not borrow to plug the gaps. A new loan to pay an old one feels like breathing room. Usually it is just delay.
  • Pick a fixed amount to live on. One line is easier than a hundred small decisions.
  • Get help if it is too much. In the UK, StepChange and National Debtline give free, confidential debt advice. In the Netherlands, your local council offers debt support and Geldfit (0800-8115) is free and anonymous.
  • Do not expect a reward. Not right away. It comes, but later than you want.

When you talk to an adviser, be honest that the debt comes from gambling. It feels scary, but it helps the people who are trying to help you. More on where to go in where to get help for gambling addiction.

After zero

What happens when the debt is gone?

At first, not much. It is a quiet moment. No fireworks.

But then you notice it. The money that went to the debt every month is yours now. For the first time in years, you are building something instead of filling a hole.

That is where I am now. I am focused on building wealth. It is one of the main things that keeps me going. Not because I want to be rich, but because I never want to go back to where I was.

Two years without a bet. Almost two years of paying off. A few months debt-free.

It is not perfect. But it is quieter.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to pay off gambling debt?

It depends on how much you owe, what you earn and what your fixed costs are. It took me almost two years to clear more than 40,000 euros, by living on a fixed amount and putting everything else towards the debt. A free debt adviser can help you work out what is realistic for you.

Should I stop gambling before paying off my debt?

Yes. While you are still gambling, anything you pay off can be lost again the next day. Stopping comes first. Self-exclusion, blocking tools and someone you trust can help.

Where can I get free help with gambling debt?

In the UK, StepChange and National Debtline offer free, confidential debt advice. In the Netherlands, contact your local council or call Geldfit on 0800-8115. Tell them honestly that the debt comes from gambling.

Is it a good idea to take out a loan to pay off gambling debt?

Usually not. A new loan to cover old debt feels like breathing room, but often just moves the problem. Get free advice first before you take on anything new.

Where to get help for gambling addiction

In crisis right now? UK: National Gambling Helpline 0808 8020 133 (free, 24/7). US: 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), call or text, 24/7. Canada: your provincial gambling helpline. Thinking about suicide? UK: Samaritans 116 123. US and Canada: call or text 988. Netherlands: OpenOverGokken 0800-2400022. Elsewhere, or in danger: your local emergency number.
