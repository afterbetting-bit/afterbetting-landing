# How much have I lost gambling? Work it out honestly

Most people who gamble do not know exactly how much they have lost. Not because they cannot work it out, but because they would rather not know.

That was me too. I gambled for 8 years. I ended up more than €40,000 in debt. Even so, I avoided looking at the total. I knew something was wrong long before I was willing to admit it. Writing the number down would have made it official.

This calculator puts the number in front of you. You enter roughly how much you lost per week and how long you have been gambling. Your answers stay in your own browser. Nobody sees them, not even us.

## Why you do not know the number

Gambling is good at hiding losses. You remember a win. A loss disappears into the next bet.

A few things happen at once:

- **You count bet by bet, not in total.** Twenty here, fifty there. On their own they feel small. Added up over months and years, they become a number you cannot ignore.
- **Wins feel bigger than losses.** One good night stays with you for years. The hundred nights before it do not.
- **Depositing does not feel like spending.** Money in a betting account feels like play money. It is your wages, your savings, or money you borrowed.
- **Not looking is easier.** As long as you do not know the number, you can tell yourself it is not that bad.

I once gambled away my whole salary in one evening. Even then I told myself I would win it back. Why that never works is in [chasing losses in gambling](https://afterbetting.com/blog/chasing-losses-gambling).

## How to enter an honest number

An estimate is fine. It does not need to be exact to the euro or the dollar. But be honest with yourself.

- **Check your bank statements.** Find the payments to betting sites. Add up a few months and divide by the number of weeks.
- **Enter what you lost, not what you staked.** Money you bet and got back does not count. Money that is gone does.
- **Include loans and overdrafts.** Money you borrowed to gamble is money you lost.
- **If in doubt, pick the higher number.** Most people underestimate.

Do this when you have a quiet moment. Not in the middle of the night, and not right after a loss.

## What the number does to you

It can land hard. A number bigger than you expected, maybe much bigger. That is normal.

For me, writing down the real amount was one of the hardest moments. It was also the moment things started to change. As long as I did not know, I could keep pretending. Once it was written down, I could not.

You do not have to do anything with it straight away. Let the number sit for a moment. What matters is that you know it now.

### If the number makes you restless

Seeing a big loss can bring up the urge to win it back. That is exactly the trap. Put your phone down, stand up, drink some water. An urge rises, peaks and fades. What to do in that moment is in [do gambling urges go away?](https://afterbetting.com/blog/do-gambling-urges-go-away)

If you feel desperate or are thinking about suicide, call or text 988 in the US and Canada, or call Samaritans on 116 123 in the UK. Free and confidential, 24/7.

## The number that actually matters

The total is the past. You cannot change it. The number that matters is what you stop losing from today.

If you lost 75 a week on average, that is 3,900 a year. Money you can keep from today. For rent, groceries, your debt, or simply a buffer.

That number grows every day you do not gamble. Every week that passes is a week that money stays yours.

## What to do with that money

### If you have debt

Put what you no longer lose towards your debt first. I lived on a fixed amount every month and everything above that went to my debt. For almost two years. The whole story is in [how I paid off my gambling debt](https://afterbetting.com/blog/paid-off-gambling-debt). For a wider plan, read [rebuilding your finances after gambling](https://afterbetting.com/blog/rebuild-finances-after-gambling).

If your debt is too big to handle alone, get free debt advice. In the UK, StepChange and National Debtline are free. In the US, look for a nonprofit credit counselling agency.

### If you have no debt

Build a small buffer first. A few hundred on an account you cannot reach easily. It gives you breathing room. And breathing room makes the urge smaller.

### If you have just gambled away your paycheck

Read [gambled away your paycheck? What now](https://afterbetting.com/blog/gambled-away-paycheck-what-now) first. It covers what to do in the next 24 hours.

## Make it harder to lose again

A number on its own will not stop you. Make it harder to deposit again.

- **Self-exclude.** GamStop in the UK, your state or provincial program in the US or Canada.
- **Switch on your bank's gambling block,** if your bank has one.
- **Block gambling sites on your phone and laptop.** How that works is in [how to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments](https://afterbetting.com/blog/block-gambling-sites-and-payments).
- **Tell one person the real number.** Saying it out loud makes it real. And you no longer carry it alone.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is what I enter stored?**
No. The calculator runs only in your own browser. We do not see your numbers and we store nothing.

**Should I enter what I staked or what I lost?**
What you lost. Money you bet and got back does not count. Look at what went from your account to betting sites, minus what came back.

**The number is much higher than I thought. What now?**
That happens to most people. You do not have to fix everything today. Talk to someone. In the UK, call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. In the US, call or text 1-800-MY-RESET. Both are free and confidential.

**Does this calculator work in pounds or dollars?**
Yes. It works the same in any currency. Enter the amounts in your own currency.
