# How to stop gambling: an honest guide

I started gambling at 16. For eight years I bet, mostly on sports. By the end I owed more than €40,000. I have now been gambling-free for more than two years, and the debt is paid off.

I am not a doctor or a therapist. I am someone who tried to stop many times, failed, and then found what actually worked. This guide is what I wish someone had told me back then.

If you are reading this at 2am with your banking app open, skip to [your first 24 hours](#first-24-hours). The rest can wait until tomorrow.

## Why can't I stop gambling?

If you have asked yourself this, you are not weak and you are not stupid. You are stuck in something that is built to keep you there.

A few things work against you at the same time:

- **The near miss.** Losing by one goal, one card or one number feels like you almost won. Your head treats it as a reason to go again.
- **Chasing losses.** Once you are down, the only way out seems to be one more bet. I once won €68,000. That became the start of years of chasing that feeling, and later of chasing what I had lost. It never works. Why it never works is in [chasing losses in gambling](https://afterbetting.com/blog/chasing-losses-gambling).
- **It is always there.** The bookmaker, the casino and the slots are on the phone in your pocket. There is no closing time.
- **Shame.** You hide it, so nobody can help. And because nobody can help, it gets worse.

I knew something was wrong long before I was willing to admit it. It crept up on me. Every step felt small enough to ignore. That is how it works for most people.

So the question is not really "why can't I stop?" The better question is "what do I need around me so stopping becomes possible?"

## How can I quit gambling? What actually works

Willpower on its own was not enough for me. A bad day, a payday or a big match always came along. What worked in the end was not one big decision. It was a few practical things, done together.

### 1. Close the doors

Make gambling hard to get to. Not impossible, because nothing is, but hard enough that the urge has time to pass.

- **Self-exclude.** In the UK, sign up to GamStop. It blocks online gambling sites licensed in Great Britain. In the US there is no single national scheme, but many states run their own self-exclusion lists.
- **Block on your devices.** Blocking software such as Gamban stops gambling sites and apps on your phone and laptop.
- **Block at your bank.** Many banks let you switch off gambling payments on your card in the app. Turn it on.

How to set all of this up is in [how to block gambling sites, apps and bank payments](https://afterbetting.com/blog/block-gambling-sites-and-payments).

### 2. Take the money out of your own hands

Money that is not there cannot be gambled. In my case, what helped most was a separate account for the bills, someone who could see what I spent, and a fixed amount each week to live on. When it was gone, it was gone.

It feels like losing control. In the beginning, that is the point.

### 3. Tell one person

Not everyone. One person. I lied for years. I said everything was fine, that it was just a distraction, and when I could not join in, I had a reason. The real reason was that there was no money.

Nobody caught me. I came clean myself. It was hard. It was also the moment the lies could stop, and the moment someone could actually help. How to have that conversation is in [how to tell your family about a gambling problem](https://afterbetting.com/blog/how-to-tell-family-about-gambling-problem).

### 4. Fill the hours you used to gamble

This is the one most guides skip, and for me it made the biggest difference.

I gambled mostly in the evenings and at weekends, when there was no work. So those were exactly the hours I had to fill. Fixed things at fixed times. Exercise. Reading about self-development. A routine that did not leave a gap for the thought that one bet would fix everything.

If you only take gambling away, you are left with an empty evening and a head full of urges. Put something in its place. More ideas in [what to do instead of gambling](https://afterbetting.com/blog/what-to-do-instead-of-gambling).

## Your first 24 hours {#first-24-hours}

If you want to stop today, do these things in this order:

1. **Stop the bleeding.** Withdraw what you can from gambling accounts, then close them.
2. **Switch on the blocks.** GamStop in the UK, the gambling block in your banking app, blocking software on your phone.
3. **Delete the apps.** Bookmakers, casinos, and the score apps that show odds.
4. **Tell one person** that today is the day. A text is enough.
5. **Plan tonight.** What are you doing at the hour you would normally bet? Write it down.
6. **Take the [gambling addiction test](https://afterbetting.com/blog/gambling-addiction-test).** Nine questions, two minutes, and nobody sees your answers. It helps to see it in black and white.

You do not need to fix your whole life today. You need to get through today without a bet.

## Can you stop gambling on your own?

Some people do. But stopping on your own does not mean stopping alone. Even if you never see a therapist, you need something to lean on: blocks, one person who knows, and a routine for the risky hours.

I had a few months of professional help. After that, routine made the difference. If you keep trying on your own and keep ending up back at the same place, that is not failure. It is a sign you need more support around you.

In the UK, call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. In the US, call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738). Both are free, confidential and open 24/7. More options, from the NHS to support groups, are in [where to get help for gambling addiction](https://afterbetting.com/blog/where-to-get-help-gambling-addiction).

## Should you quit cold turkey?

With gambling, stopping completely works better than cutting down. Every small bet keeps the idea alive that this time will be different. For me it never started big. The big amounts came later.

Stopping in one go is hard, though. The first weeks were the toughest for me. The urges and the emptiness. Triggers I did not see coming. Waking up at night with one thought. After a few weeks it got better, and my sleep came back.

What to expect in those first days is in [your first week without gambling](https://afterbetting.com/blog/first-week-without-gambling) and [gambling withdrawal symptoms](https://afterbetting.com/blog/gambling-withdrawal-symptoms).

## How to stop gambling online and on your phone

Most gambling now happens on a phone. That makes it easy to start and hard to stop, because the casino is with you on the sofa, in bed and on the toilet.

A few things that help:

- **Make the phone boring.** Delete the apps, log out of everything, and remove saved cards from your browser.
- **Turn off the triggers.** Unsubscribe from gambling emails and texts, and turn off notifications from score apps.
- **Keep the phone out of the bedroom.** Late at night, tired and alone, is when the worst decisions happen.
- **Watch out for alcohol.** For me, drinking made the triggers worse. A few drinks lowered every barrier I had built.

If you mainly bet on sport, the matches never stop coming. What helped me there is in [how to quit sports betting](https://afterbetting.com/blog/sports-betting-addiction-quit).

## When the urge hits

Urges come in waves. They build, peak and then fade, often within twenty minutes, if you do not act on them.

When it comes:

- **Delay by 15 minutes.** Set a timer. You are not deciding never. Just not now.
- **Move.** Another room, outside, a walk around the block.
- **Play the film to the end.** Not to the win, but to the night after, and the week after. Where are you? What is in your account?
- **Text someone.** "I am having a hard moment" is enough.

More on getting through the evenings in [how to stop gambling urges at night and on weekends](https://afterbetting.com/blog/how-to-stop-gambling-urges-weekends).

## If you slip

After one of my first attempts to stop, I relapsed. A lot of people do. It does not mean you cannot do it, and it does not wipe out the days you had.

What matters is what you do next. Tell someone, switch the blocks back on, look at what led up to it, and start again today rather than next Monday. There is a step-by-step plan in [what to do right after a gambling relapse](https://afterbetting.com/blog/what-to-do-after-gambling-relapse).

## What about the debt?

The debt does not disappear when the gambling stops. Mine was on credit cards, personal loans, and money borrowed from people close to me. It took me almost two years to pay it off. I lived on a fixed amount every month, and everything above that went to the debt.

The hardest part was working hard without seeing anything back straight away. But every month the number got smaller, and so did the pull to win it back. How I did it is in [how I paid off €40k of gambling debt](https://afterbetting.com/blog/paid-off-gambling-debt). In the UK, StepChange and National Debtline give free debt advice.

## What changes when you stop

It does not happen overnight, and nobody hands you a reward for it. But things change.

My sleep came back after a few weeks. The lying stopped, because there was nothing left to hide. The debt went down every month instead of up. And slowly the evenings and weekends became mine again, instead of belonging to the next match or the next spin.

The urge did not vanish on a fixed date. It came less often, and when it came, it had less grip. I have now been gambling-free for more than two years. I am not special. I just stopped relying on willpower and built something around me that made gambling harder and the rest of life easier.

If you are at the start, that is the part to hold on to. You do not have to feel ready. You only have to take the next step today.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best way to stop gambling?**
There is no single best way. What helps almost everyone is a combination: stop completely, block access with self-exclusion and blocking software, take the money out of your own hands for a while, tell one person, and fill the hours you used to gamble.

**Can you stop gambling on your own?**
Some people do. But even without treatment, you need support around you: blocks, one person who knows, and a routine. If you keep ending up back at the same point, call a helpline. That is a step, not a failure.

**How long does it take to stop gambling?**
The first weeks are usually the hardest. Urges become less frequent and less intense over time, but they can come back with triggers for a long while. More in [how long gambling recovery takes](https://afterbetting.com/blog/how-long-gambling-recovery-takes).

**Is there an app to help you stop gambling?**
Yes. Blocking apps such as Gamban stop gambling sites. Afterbetting helps with what comes after the block: a day counter, habits, a journal, a money overview and Help now. The basics are free.

**Why is it so hard to stop gambling?**
Because several things pull at you at once: near misses that feel like almost winning, losses you want to win back, gambling that is always on your phone, and shame that keeps you quiet. Knowing that does not make it easy, but it does make it less personal.
